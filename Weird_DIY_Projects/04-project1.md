# Chapter 4: Project - The Self-Watering Sock Monster Planter

Welcome to your first official Weird DIY project! We're starting with a classic combination of goofy, slightly creepy, and surprisingly functional: The Self-Watering Sock Monster Planter. This little critter will not only add a touch of weirdness to your windowsill but will also help keep your small plants hydrated. It’s the perfect guardian for your herbs or tiny succulents, assuming it doesn't develop a taste for them first.

**Concept:** A planter made from a sock, decorated to look like a monster, with a simple wicking system that draws water up from a reservoir to keep the soil moist.

## Materials & Tools

**For the Monster & Planter:**

*   **One Old Sock:** The thicker, the better (like an athletic sock or a fluffy winter sock). Any color or pattern will do – weird is the goal! Ensure it's clean, please.
*   **Small Plastic Pot or Yogurt Cup:** This will sit inside the sock to hold the soil and plant. It should be small enough to fit comfortably inside the sock with some room around it.
*   **Googly Eyes (Various Sizes):** Essential for any self-respecting monster.
*   **Felt Scraps (Various Colors):** For teeth, tongues, horns, spots, etc.
*   **Yarn or String:** For hair, warts, or decorative stitching.
*   **Strong Craft Glue or Hot Glue Gun:** For attaching features.
*   **Scissors.**
*   **Permanent Marker.**

**For the Self-Watering System:**

*   **A Small Water Reservoir:** This could be a small glass jar, a plastic cup, or the bottom of a plastic bottle. It needs to be shorter than the sock monster but wide enough to be stable.
*   **Thick Cotton String, Yarn, or a Strip of Felt/Old T-shirt Fabric:** This will be your wick. About 6-8 inches long.
*   **Soil.**
*   **A Small Plant:** Herbs like basil or mint, or small succulents work well.

## Step-by-Step Construction

**Part 1: Creating the Sock Monster Body**

1.  **Prepare the Sock:** Turn the sock inside out. If your sock is very long, you might want to cut it down a bit from the opening. You want it to be tall enough to cover the inner plastic pot and have a little extra "neck."
2.  **Shape the Head (Optional):** If you want a more defined head shape (e.g., rounded or with ears), you can sew a simple seam across the toe area while it's inside out. For a basic monster, the natural toe shape is fine.
    *   *For simple ears:* Pinch two small sections at the toe end and secure them with a few stitches or a small rubber band on the inside. Turn right-side out to see your floppy ears!
3.  **Turn Right-Side Out:** Your basic monster form is ready!

**Part 2: Assembling the Planter & Wicking System**

1.  **Prepare the Inner Pot:**
    *   Take your small plastic pot or yogurt cup. If it doesn't have drainage holes, carefully poke a few small holes in the bottom using scissors or a skewer (adult supervision if needed!). This is important.
    *   Poke one slightly larger hole in the center of the bottom of this inner pot – large enough for your wick (cotton string/yarn/fabric strip) to pass through.
2.  **Insert the Wick:**
    *   Thread your wick material through the central hole in the inner pot.
    *   Leave about 2-3 inches of the wick inside the pot, coiling it loosely at the bottom.
    *   The rest of the wick should dangle underneath the pot (this part will go into the water reservoir).
3.  **Place Inner Pot in Sock:** Carefully slide the inner pot (with wick dangling) into the sock, pushing it down towards the toe end. The top rim of the inner pot should be a little below the opening of the sock, creating a "collar" of sock material.
4.  **Add Soil & Plant:**
    *   Gently fill the inner pot with soil, making sure the wick stays somewhat centered and covered at the bottom.
    *   Plant your chosen small plant in the soil.
    *   You can now fold the top edge of the sock down over the rim of the inner pot, or leave it up like a floppy neck.

**Part 3: Monstrous Makeover!**

This is where your creature comes to life!

1.  **Eyes are Everything:** Glue on your googly eyes. One giant eye? Multiple tiny eyes? Eyes on stalks (use pipe cleaners glued to eyes, then to the sock)? Go wild!
2.  **Terrifying Teeth (or a Goofy Grin):**
    *   Cut out teeth shapes from felt (triangles for scary, squares for buck-toothed).
    *   Glue them around the "mouth" area. This could be the toe seam, or you could define a mouth by slightly pinching and gluing a section of the sock.
3.  **Luscious Locks (or Mangy Mops):**
    *   Cut lengths of yarn. Glue them on for hair. A wild tuft on top? Flowing sideburns?
4.  **Horns, Tongues, and Other Appendages:**
    *   Cut out felt shapes for horns, a long floppy tongue, spots, warts, or tiny monster arms.
    *   Glue these onto your monster.
5.  **Finishing Touches:** Use permanent markers to add details like nostrils, scars, or pupils if your googly eyes are plain.

**Part 4: Setting Up the Self-Watering System**

1.  **Fill the Reservoir:** Add water to your chosen water reservoir (jar or plastic cup), leaving about an inch of space at the top.
2.  **Assemble:**
    *   Place the water reservoir where your plant will live.
    *   Carefully place your sock monster planter on top of the reservoir, ensuring the wick dangling from the bottom of the sock monster is submerged in the water. The bottom of the sock monster itself should NOT be sitting in the water, only the wick.
    *   The wick will slowly draw water up into the soil, keeping it moist for your plant.

**Care and Feeding of Your Monster:**

*   Refill the water reservoir as needed.
*   Keep an eye on the soil moisture. If it seems too wet or too dry, you might need to adjust the length of the wick in the water or the type of wick material.
*   Give your monster a name. It deserves one.

## Customization Corner: Give Your Monster Personality!

*   **Sock Choice:** Striped socks make for stripy monsters, argyle for sophisticated monsters, fluffy socks for cuddly (but still terrifying) monsters.
*   **Eye Variations:** Use buttons, beads, or even painted ping pong ball halves for different eye effects.
*   **Monster Theme:** Could it be a vampire monster (add felt fangs and a tiny cape)? A zombie monster (use a holey sock and some greenish felt)? A disco monster (add sequins!)?
*   **Multiple Heads:** Feeling ambitious? Try to attach a smaller sock "head" to the side.

## Challenge Your Weirdness: Advanced Sock Monster Features

1.  **Articulated Limbs:** Create simple arms from pipe cleaners wrapped in felt or yarn and stitch/glue them to the sides.
2.  **Sound Effects:** Sew in a small squeaker (from a pet toy) that makes a noise when the monster is "poked."
3.  **Glow-in-the-Dark:** Use glow-in-the-dark paint for eyes or teeth for a spooky nighttime effect.

Congratulations! You've created your very own Self-Watering Sock Monster Planter. May it thrive and bring a touch of delightful weirdness to your life. Don't be surprised if you find yourself making a whole army of them.

### Chapter Summary

*   This project creates a functional self-watering planter disguised as a quirky sock monster.
*   Key materials include a sock, a small inner pot, googly eyes, felt, yarn, and a wicking material.
*   Construction involves creating the monster body, assembling the wicking planter, decorating the monster, and setting it up on a water reservoir.
*   Customization options are plentiful, from sock choice to thematic monster designs.

### Action Steps/Challenges

1.  **Gather Your Socks:** Raid your sock drawer (or a family member's, with permission!) for potential monster candidates. Look for interesting textures and colors.
2.  **Sketch Your Monster:** Before you start cutting and gluing, sketch a few different monster face ideas. Experiment with eye placement, teeth shapes, and hairstyles.
3.  **Name Brainstorm:** While you work, think of at least three suitably weird names for your sock monster.
4.  **Find the Perfect Plant:** Consider what small plant would best suit your monster's personality. A spiky succulent for a fierce monster? A delicate fern for a shy one?
