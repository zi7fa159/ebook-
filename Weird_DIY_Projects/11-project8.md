# Chapter 11: Project - The Desktop Catapult (For Office "Supply" Warfare)

Warning! This project may lead to increased office morale, spontaneous bouts of laughter, and the occasional misfired marshmallow. We're building a Desktop Catapult, a marvel of miniature medieval engineering perfect for launching soft projectiles across your desk, cubicle, or (if you're daring) into the recycling bin with dramatic flair. It's time to turn everyday office supplies into instruments of playful siege!

**Concept:** A small, functional catapult built primarily from craft sticks (popsicle sticks), rubber bands, and a bottle cap, capable of launching lightweight items like paper wads, marshmallows, or pom-poms.

**Disclaimer:** This is for FUN. Please use responsibly. Aim away from faces, fragile objects, and anyone who doesn't appreciate a surprise cotton ball attack. Check your workplace's policy on fun before engaging in full-scale cubicle crusades. AI Publishing Pro is not responsible for any HR incidents or lost marshmallows.

## Materials & Tools

*   **Craft Sticks (Popsicle Sticks):** Standard size. You'll need about 8-10.
*   **Rubber Bands:** Various sizes, but you'll need at least 3-4 good quality ones that aren't too brittle. Standard office rubber bands work well.
*   **Plastic Bottle Cap:** This will be your launching bucket. A milk jug cap or soda bottle cap is ideal.
*   **Hot Glue Gun or Strong Craft Glue:** For securing the bottle cap and potentially reinforcing joints.
*   **Optional: Small Wooden Dowel or Skewer:** Can be used for a more robust fulcrum if you want to get fancy, but not strictly necessary for the basic design.
*   **Optional: Binder Clip (Small or Medium):** Can be used as a trigger or release mechanism for more advanced designs.
*   **Ammunition:** Small paper wads, mini marshmallows, cotton balls, pom-poms, foam earplugs. Keep it lightweight and soft!

## Step-by-Step Catapult Construction

This is a common and effective design for a small craft stick catapult.

**Part 1: Building the Base Frame**

1.  **Create the Main Stack:** Take 6 craft sticks (or 7 for a thicker base). Stack them neatly one on top of the other.
2.  **Secure the Stack:** Tightly wrap a rubber band around one end of the stack, about 1/2 inch from the edge. Do the same on the other end. This stack is the stable base of your catapult.

**Part 2: Creating the Fulcrum and Lever Arm**

1.  **Prepare the Lever Arm Sticks:** Take 2 craft sticks.
2.  **Create the Fulcrum Point:**
    *   Slide one of these single craft sticks *between* the bottom-most stick and the rest of the main stack (the stack of 6 you just made). Position it perpendicular to the main stack, about one-third of the way from one end. This stick will act as a pivot point or fulcrum. (See diagram/imagine this).
    *   *Alternative Fulcrum:* Some designs place this cross-stick *on top* of the main stack, which can also work. Experiment to see what you prefer. For this guide, we'll stick with sliding it into the stack.
3.  **Position the Firing Arm:**
    *   Place the second single craft stick (this will be your main firing arm) on top of the main stack, aligned with it, so one end extends out beyond the stack. This extending end will hold your launching bucket.
    *   The other end of this firing arm stick should rest *on top* of the fulcrum stick you inserted in step 2.

**Part 3: Assembling the Catapult Mechanism**

1.  **Secure the Firing Arm to the Fulcrum:**
    *   This is the trickiest part to get right for good tension. You need to use rubber bands to create a "V" shape that pulls the firing arm down onto the fulcrum and connects it to the base stack.
    *   Criss-cross one or two rubber bands around the firing arm and the main base stack, ensuring they go *over* the firing arm and *under* the main base stack, effectively clamping the firing arm down onto the fulcrum stick.
    *   The tension here is key. Too loose, and you won't get much power. Too tight, and it might break the sticks or be hard to operate.
    *   It should look something like this from the side:
        *   Firing Arm on top: `___________`
        *   Fulcrum Stick: `    | |` (perpendicular underneath firing arm, on top of base)
        *   Base Stack: `    XXXXX` (rubber bands crisscrossed)
        *   `   ======` (main base stack of 6)

    *   Adjust the position of the fulcrum stick (the one you inserted) forward or backward along the base stack to change the lever mechanics. Closer to the "front" (launching end) usually gives more power but less range of motion.

**Part 4: Attaching the Launching Bucket**

1.  **Glue the Bottle Cap:** Take your plastic bottle cap. Apply hot glue or strong craft glue to its bottom.
2.  **Position on Firing Arm:** Press the bottle cap firmly onto the end of the firing arm that extends out. Ensure it's centered and secure. Let the glue dry completely if not using hot glue.

**Part 5: Testing and Refining**

1.  **Load Your Ammo:** Place a lightweight projectile (like a mini marshmallow) into the bottle cap.
2.  **Launch!**
    *   Hold the base of the catapult firmly with one hand.
    *   With your other hand, press down on the tip of the firing arm (the end with the bottle cap).
    *   Release quickly!
3.  **Troubleshooting & Adjustments:**
    *   **Not Enough Power?**
        *   Try adding another rubber band to the criss-cross mechanism for more tension.
        *   Adjust the position of the fulcrum stick. Moving it closer to the end where you press down can sometimes increase leverage.
        *   Ensure your rubber bands aren't old and stretched out.
    *   **Firing Arm Wobbly?** Make sure the rubber bands securing it are tight and well-placed.
    *   **Sticks Breaking?** You might have too much tension, or your craft sticks might be flimsy. Double up on the firing arm stick if needed (glue two together for strength).
    *   **Inaccurate Shots?** Practice your aim! Also, ensure the catapult is on a stable, flat surface.

## Customization Corner: Targeting Systems and Ammo Types

*   **"Laser" Sight (Decorative):** Glue a red plastic straw or a painted coffee stirrer along the firing arm to look like a targeting laser. (It won't actually help aim, but it looks cool!).
*   **Team Colors:** Use colored craft sticks or paint your catapult in your favorite "team" colors.
*   **Ammo Holder:** Glue a second bottle cap or a small felt pouch to the base of the catapult to hold extra ammunition.
*   **Different Projectiles:**
    *   **Pom-poms:** Soft and fluffy, good for indoor use.
    *   **Eraser Heads (Small):** A bit more weight, might fly further.
    *   **Rolled-up Post-it Notes:** Customizable messages for your targets!

## Challenge Your Weirdness: Accuracy and Distance Competitions

1.  **Target Practice:**
    *   Set up a target: a small cup, a designated square on a piece of paper, or an unsuspecting (but consenting) rubber duck.
    *   See how many shots it takes to hit the target from a set distance.
2.  **Distance Challenge:**
    *   Mark a starting line.
    *   See who can launch their projectile the farthest. Use a measuring tape.
    *   Experiment with different ammo types to see which flies best.
3.  **Trick Shots:**
    *   Can you launch a marshmallow into a cup held by a friend? (Friend should wear safety glasses, just in case!).
    *   Can you bounce a pom-pom off a wall and into a target?

2.  **Fulcrum Experiment (Mental or Physical):** Think about how a lever works. If you move the fulcrum (the pivot point) closer to the load (the bottle cap), what happens? If you move it closer to where you apply force, what changes?
3.  **Ammo Test:** Gather a few different lightweight items that could be used as ammo. Which do you predict will fly the farthest or most accurately from your catapult?
4.  **Target Designation:** Identify a safe, inanimate target in your workspace or home that will be the first recipient of a projectile from your completed catapult. (A wastepaper bin is a classic choice).
