# Chapter 1: The First Test

The summons came before dawn. A blaring siren, followed by a synthesized voice echoing through the D-Block: “All D-personnel, report to Briefing Room Gamma. Five minutes.”

D-9341 was already awake, the thin mattress offering little comfort. He’d spent the night, or what passed for it in the windowless confines of Site-113, listening to the unsettling symphony of the facility – distant clangs, the hum of machinery, the occasional, unidentifiable screech that sent shivers down his spine. Sleep had been a shallow, restless affair, populated by fragmented nightmares of shadowy figures and impossible geometries.

He joined the shuffling line of orange-clad figures, their faces pale and drawn in the harsh fluorescent light of the corridor. Guards, their expressions hidden behind dark visors, flanked them, prods held ready. The atmosphere was thick with a mixture of resignation and raw fear.

Briefing Room Gamma was another sterile, functional space, dominated by a large projection screen. Dr. Aris Thorne stood at the front, the same impeccably dressed, unnervingly calm figure from their initial processing. With him were two armed guards and a woman in a lab coat, her blonde hair pulled back severely, her eyes sharp and intelligent. Her name tag read ‘Dr. Eva Rostova, Anomalous Biology’.

“Good morning, D-Class,” Thorne began, his voice smooth and devoid of inflection. “Today, you will participate in your first practical assessment. This will involve interaction with a newly acquired anomalous entity, designated SCP-XXXX.”

A murmur rippled through the assembled D-Class. SCP-XXXX. The designation sounded ominous, clinical.

Dr. Rostova stepped forward. “SCP-XXXX is a Class 3 Keter-level biological entity,” she stated, her tone crisp. “Its primary anomalous property appears to be rapid, uncontrolled morphogenesis, influenced by proximity to biological subjects and, we suspect, strong emotional states. In layman’s terms, it changes. How it changes, and into what, is what we are here to determine.”

On the screen behind her, a grainy, black-and-white video began to play. It showed a containment cell, empty at first. Then, from a reinforced hatch in the floor, a… *thing* was introduced. It was difficult to describe. Initially, it resembled a large, quivering mass of translucent, gelatinous flesh, roughly ovoid in shape. As the camera zoomed in, D-9341 could see faint, pulsing veins beneath its surface, and what looked like multiple, mismatched eyes that opened and closed sporadically.

“This is SCP-XXXX in its baseline state,” Rostova explained. “Observe.”

A single D-Class, looking terrified, was then introduced into the cell in the video. As he approached the entity, it began to shift. Tendrils of flesh erupted from its surface, some tipped with razor-sharp claws, others with suckers that writhed like hungry mouths. The D-Class screamed, trying to back away, but the entity was surprisingly fast, lunging forward. The video cut out abruptly, just as the tendrils made contact.

A wave of nausea hit D-9341. Keter-level. Changes. That thing was a nightmare given form.

“Your task today is simple,” Thorne said, taking over from Rostova. “You will enter SCP-XXXX’s containment chamber in groups of three. You will be equipped with standard D-Class gear – a jumpsuit, basic helmet, and a shoulder-mounted telemetry unit. No weapons. Your objective is to approach SCP-XXXX, observe its transformations, and attempt to interact with it verbally. Any and all reactions are to be reported to the observation team via your telemetry units.”

“Interact with *that*?” someone muttered from the back, voice trembling.

Thorne’s gaze, cold as glacial ice, swept towards the sound. “Compliance is mandatory. Non-compliance will be… counterproductive to your continued well-being.” He smiled faintly, a chillingly predatory expression. “The first group will consist of D-9341, D-2877, and D-7650.”

D-9341’s stomach tightened. He glanced at the two others Thorne had named. D-2877 was a woman, small and wiry, with intelligent, darting eyes that betrayed her fear. Her knuckles were white where she gripped the edge of her seat. D-7650 was a big man, powerfully built, his face a stoic mask, but D-9341 could see the tension in his jaw.

“You have ten minutes to prepare,” Thorne announced. “Proceed to staging area Beta-7.”

The walk to Beta-7 was silent, the three of them lost in their own grim thoughts. Staging Area Beta-7 was a small antechamber adjoining a heavy, reinforced steel door labeled ‘SCP-XXXX – CONTAINMENT CHAMBER 3’. Two technicians in hazmat suits helped them don their helmets and checked their telemetry units. The helmets were claustrophobic, the recycled air smelling faintly of chemicals.

“Remember,” one technician said, his voice tinny through the helmet’s comms, “Maintain verbal contact. Describe everything you see, everything you feel. Your observations are critical.”

D-9341 nodded, his mouth dry. He looked at Lena (D-2877) and Javi (D-7650). Lena gave a shaky, almost imperceptible nod. Javi just stared at the door, his expression unreadable.

“Ready when you are, D-Class,” Thorne’s voice crackled in their ears, presumably from an observation room.

The heavy door hissed open, revealing a brightly lit, white-tiled chamber. It was larger than it looked in the video, perhaps twenty meters square. In the center, under a focused spotlight, pulsed SCP-XXXX. It was larger now, too, easily the size of a small car, its gelatinous surface rippling. The mismatched eyes blinked slowly, focusing on them as they stepped inside.

The door slammed shut behind them with a deafening clang, the sound of a tomb sealing.

“D-9341, proceed first,” Thorne’s voice commanded.

Taking a deep breath, D-9341 started walking towards the entity. The air in the chamber was cold, but he could feel sweat prickling under his jumpsuit. Each step felt like a mile.

“Approaching SCP-XXXX,” he reported, his voice surprisingly steady. “It’s… larger than in the briefing footage. Surface is… undulating. Multiple eyes, various sizes, all tracking my movement.”

As he got closer, about ten meters away, the entity began to change. The smooth, gelatinous surface started to bulge and contort. Long, thin pseudopods, like strands of molten glass, extruded from its mass, twitching and exploring the air.

“It’s reacting to my proximity,” D-9341 said. “Growing… appendages. They look like… glass? No, more like crystal. Sharp.”

“D-2877, D-7650, advance to five meters,” Thorne ordered.

Lena and Javi moved up, flanking D-9341 slightly. As they did, the entity’s transformation accelerated. The crystalline shards grew longer, thicker, forming a defensive-looking barrier around its core. A low hum emanated from it, vibrating through the floor and into D-9341’s boots.

“Subject D-2877 reporting,” Lena’s voice was tight, but she kept it even. “The entity is forming crystalline structures. They appear to be silica-based, highly reflective. Temperature in their immediate vicinity is dropping.”

“It’s making a noise,” Javi added, his voice a low rumble. “A hum. Getting louder. Feels like it’s in my teeth.”

Suddenly, one of the crystalline shards detached and shot towards D-9341 with incredible speed. He yelped, instinctively flinging himself to the side. The shard embedded itself in the wall where he’d been standing, shattering into a thousand tiny pieces.

“Hostile reaction!” D-9341 shouted into his comm. “It fired a projectile! Crystal shard!”

“Noted, D-9341,” Thorne’s voice was infuriatingly calm. “Continue observation. Attempt verbal communication.”

Verbal communication? With that thing? D-9341 felt a surge of anger, quickly suppressed. He had to stay focused.

“SCP-XXXX,” he said, trying to keep his voice level. “Can you understand me? We are not here to harm you.”

The entity pulsed. More shards formed, larger this time, angling towards them like a battery of deadly spears. The hum intensified, becoming a painful throb in their heads.

“It doesn’t seem receptive to conversation, Doctor,” D-9341 said dryly.

“Perhaps a different emotional stimulus is required,” Rostova’s voice cut in. “D-7650, display aggression. Shout at it. Threaten it.”

Javi looked incredulous, but a moment later, his face hardened. He stepped forward, drawing himself up to his full height. “Hey, you ugly bastard!” he roared, his voice booming even through the helmet comms. “You want a piece of us? Come on, then! Show us what you got!”

The effect was instantaneous and terrifying. The crystalline structures retracted, melting back into the entity’s gelatinous mass. But then, its entire form began to swell and darken. The mismatched eyes turned a furious, glowing red. Deep fissures appeared on its surface, like cracks in cooling lava, and from within them, an intense heat began to radiate.

“What’s it doing?” Lena stammered, backing away.

“It’s… it’s changing again,” D-9341 reported, his voice strained. “Color shifting to a dark red, almost black. Emitting heat. Significant heat.”

The entity let out a sound then, not a hum, but a guttural, wet roar that seemed to shake the very foundations of the chamber. Its surface bubbled, and then, with horrifying speed, it launched a pseudopod of superheated, molten flesh directly at Javi.
