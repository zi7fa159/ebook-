# Chapter 6: Step-by-Step: Building a Media Bed Guppy System

With your planning complete, it's time for the most exciting part: building your very own media bed guppy aquaponic system! This chapter will provide a step-by-step guide to assembling a common beginner-friendly setup. Remember to refer back to your plans from Chapter 5. We'll assume you're building a system with a fish tank and a grow bed positioned above it, using a bell siphon for a flood-and-drain cycle.

**Safety First!**
*   Always work in a well-lit area.
*   If using tools, ensure you know how to operate them safely.
*   Be careful when lifting heavy objects.
*   When working with plumbing, expect minor leaks initially – it's part of the process! Have towels ready.
*   Ensure all electrical components are kept away from direct water contact until the system is confirmed leak-free and running. Use GFCI outlets.

## Gathering Your Materials and Tools

Before you begin, lay out all your components and tools. This is what you'll typically need (refer to your specific shopping list from Chapter 5):

**Components:**
*   Fish Tank (e.g., 10-20 gallon tote or aquarium)
*   Grow Bed Container (e.g., slightly larger/equivalent volume tote)
*   Grow Media (e.g., LECA, lava rock – enough to fill grow bed to 10-12 inches depth)
*   Submersible Water Pump (appropriate GPH for your tank size)
*   Air Pump, Airline Tubing, and Air Stone
*   Plumbing Parts:
    *   PVC pipes (for standpipe, water inlet, potentially supports)
    *   Vinyl tubing (to connect pump to grow bed)
    *   Bulkhead fitting (to create a watertight seal for the drain in the grow bed)
    *   PVC fittings (male/female adapters, elbows, tees, reducers as needed for your design)
    *   Bell Siphon components:
        *   Larger diameter PVC pipe for the "bell"
        *   Smaller diameter PVC pipe for the "standpipe"
        *   Cap or reducer for the top of the bell (with a small air hole)
        *   Gravel guard (larger pipe section with slits or a plastic mesh container)
    *   Optional: Ball valve for flow control from the pump.
*   Optional: Aquarium heater (if needed)
*   Optional: Support structure for the grow bed if it's not sitting directly on the fish tank (e.g., sturdy shelving, wooden frame).

**Tools:**
*   Drill with various bit sizes (including a hole saw bit for the bulkhead fitting)
*   PVC pipe cutter or hacksaw
*   Measuring tape
*   Level
*   Sandpaper or deburring tool (to smooth cut PVC edges)
*   Silicone sealant (aquarium-safe, if needed for any non-standard seals, though bulkheads should be watertight)
*   Screwdrivers (if assembling a stand)
*   Buckets (for washing media)
*   Safety glasses

## Step 1: Preparing the Fish Tank

1.  **Clean the Tank**: Thoroughly clean your chosen fish tank with water and a clean cloth or sponge. Avoid soaps or detergents, as residues can harm fish. If using a pre-owned tank, a diluted vinegar solution can help remove mineral deposits, followed by copious rinsing.
2.  **Position the Tank**: Place the fish tank in its final designated location. Ensure it's level and stable.
3.  **Install Air Stone and Heater (if using)**:
    *   Connect the airline tubing to the air stone and the air pump. Place the air stone in the tank. Position the air pump *above* the water level of the tank or use a check valve in the airline tubing to prevent water from siphoning back into the pump if the power fails.
    *   If using a heater, install it according to the manufacturer's instructions, but **do not plug it in yet**.

## Step 2: Constructing or Preparing the Grow Bed

1.  **Clean the Grow Bed**: Just like the fish tank, thoroughly clean the grow bed container.
2.  **Drill Drain Hole for Bulkhead**:
    *   Determine the location for your drain (where the bell siphon will sit). This is typically offset from the center to allow even water distribution.
    *   Carefully drill a hole sized appropriately for your bulkhead fitting. Start with a smaller pilot hole if you're unsure.
3.  **Install Bulkhead Fitting**:
    *   Insert the bulkhead fitting through the hole, ensuring rubber gaskets are correctly positioned (usually one inside, one outside) to create a watertight seal. Tighten securely but don't overtighten to crack the plastic.
4.  **Position the Grow Bed**:
    *   Place the grow bed above the fish tank. It can sit directly on top if the tank design allows and is sturdy, or on a dedicated stand.
    *   Ensure there's enough space for water to drain from the grow bed back into the fish tank without splashing excessively. The drain outlet should be positioned over the fish tank.

## Step 3: Assembling and Installing the Bell Siphon

The bell siphon is the heart of a media bed's flood-and-drain cycle.

1.  **Cut Standpipe**:
    *   The standpipe (smaller diameter PVC) attaches to the inside of the bulkhead fitting. Its height determines the maximum water level in your grow bed. A common height is about 1-2 inches below the top of your grow media.
2.  **Construct Gravel Guard**:
    *   This is a wider piece of PVC pipe with many slits or holes drilled into its lower half, or a sturdy mesh cylinder. It fits around the standpipe and prevents grow media from clogging the siphon or drain. It should be taller than the standpipe.
3.  **Construct the Bell**:
    *   The bell is a larger diameter PVC pipe, capped at the top, that fits loosely over the standpipe and gravel guard.
    *   The bottom of the bell should have small notches or be slightly elevated (e.g., by small PVC feet) to allow water to enter from the bottom.
    *   Drill a tiny air hole (e.g., 1/16" or 1-2mm) near the top of the bell cap or on the side of the bell near the top. This helps break the siphon once the water level drops.
4.  **Assemble in Grow Bed**:
    *   Screw the standpipe into the bulkhead fitting from inside the grow bed.
    *   Place the gravel guard around the standpipe.
    *   Place the bell over the gravel guard and standpipe.
    *   *(Detailed diagrams/videos are very helpful for understanding bell siphon construction and tuning – recommend readers search for these visuals.)*

## Step 4: Setting up the Plumbing: Pump to Grow Bed

1.  **Position Water Pump**: Place the submersible pump in the fish tank, away from the direct path of the draining water from the grow bed to avoid sucking in too much debris.
2.  **Connect Tubing to Pump**: Attach one end of your flexible vinyl tubing to the pump's outlet.
3.  **Run Tubing to Grow Bed**: Route the tubing up to the grow bed.
4.  **Create Water Inlet**: Secure the other end of the tubing to deliver water into the grow bed. You might use a simple U-bend of PVC or just let the tube empty at one end of the bed. Ensure the water inlet is positioned to encourage even water distribution across the media.
5.  **Install Ball Valve (Optional)**: If your pump is powerful, install an inline ball valve on the tubing before it reaches the grow bed to control the flow rate.

## Step 5: Washing and Adding Grow Media

This is a crucial but often messy step!

1.  **Thoroughly Rinse Grow Media**: Grow media (especially LECA and lava rock) comes with a lot of dust and fine particles that can cloud your water and clog your system.
    *   Place batches of media in a bucket or colander and rinse repeatedly with a hose or tap water until the water runs clear. This may take several rinses.
2.  **Add Media to Grow Bed**: Carefully add the rinsed grow media to the grow bed, distributing it evenly around the bell siphon assembly. Fill to a depth of 10-12 inches. Be careful not to dislodge the siphon components.

## Step 6: Leak Testing Your System

Before adding fish or fully committing to cycling, test for leaks.

1.  **Fill Fish Tank**: Add some water to the fish tank – enough to submerge the pump. **Use dechlorinated water if you plan to proceed to cycling soon, or tap water if it's just a quick mechanical test (you'll discard and refill with dechlorinated water later).** To dechlorinate, let tap water sit for 24-48 hours, or use an aquarium water conditioner.
2.  **Turn on the Pump**: Plug in the water pump. Water should start filling the grow bed.
3.  **Observe**:
    *   Check all plumbing connections for leaks (bulkhead, pipe fittings, tubing connections). Tighten or adjust as needed.
    *   Watch the grow bed fill. Ensure the bell siphon engages correctly once the water reaches the top of the standpipe and drains the bed.
    *   Observe the siphon breaking and the cycle repeating.
4.  **Troubleshoot Siphon (If Necessary)**: Bell siphons can be finicky to tune initially.
    *   *Siphon won't start*: Check for air leaks in the bell, ensure water flow isn't too low, ensure the bell isn't sealed too tightly at the bottom.
    *   *Siphon won't break*: The air hole at the top of the bell might be too small or clogged, or the drain rate might be too perfectly matched to the fill rate.
    *   Adjust flow rate, bell height, or air hole size as needed. Patience is key!

## Step 7: Initial System Cycling (The Fishless Cycle - IMPORTANT!)

Once your system is assembled, leak-free, and the siphon is working, you **must cycle it before adding fish**. This process establishes the beneficial bacterial colonies needed for the nitrogen cycle.

*   **Why Fishless Cycling is Crucial**: Adding fish to an uncycled system will expose them to toxic ammonia and nitrite spikes as the bacteria colonies are not yet established enough to process the waste. This is stressful and often fatal to fish.
*   **Methods for Fishless Cycling (Adding an Ammonia Source)**:
    1.  **Pure Ammonia**: Add a few drops of unscented, pure liquid ammonia (no surfactants or additives – check the label!) to the fish tank to achieve an initial ammonia reading of 2-4 ppm (parts per million) on your test kit.
    2.  **Fish Food**: Add a small pinch of fish food to the tank daily. As it decomposes, it will release ammonia. This method is slower.
    3.  **Seeding Material (Optional but Recommended)**: If you have access to established aquarium filter media, gravel from a healthy tank, or a bottled bacteria starter (use reputable aquarium brands), adding this can significantly speed up the cycling process.
*   **Tracking Progress with Water Tests**:
    1.  **Daily Testing**: Test for ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate daily.
    2.  **Ammonia Rises, then Falls**: You'll see ammonia levels rise first.
    3.  **Nitrite Rises, then Falls**: As Nitrosomonas bacteria establish, ammonia will drop, and nitrite levels will rise.
    4.  **Nitrate Rises**: As Nitrobacter bacteria establish, nitrite levels will drop, and nitrate levels will begin to rise.
    5.  **Cycling is Complete When**: You can add a dose of ammonia (e.g., to 2 ppm), and within 24 hours, both ammonia and nitrite test at 0 ppm, while nitrates are present. This process can take anywhere from **2 to 8 weeks**, depending on temperature and method.
*   **Maintain pH and Temperature**: Keep pH in the 6.8-7.5 range and temperature warm (75-80°F / 24-27°C) during cycling to encourage bacterial growth.
*   **Do Not Add Fish Until Cycled!**

Building your first aquaponic system is a hands-on learning experience. Take your time, follow the steps, and don't be discouraged by minor setbacks. The reward of a functioning ecosystem you built yourself is well worth the effort. In the next chapter, we'll finally introduce your guppies to their new, fully cycled home!
