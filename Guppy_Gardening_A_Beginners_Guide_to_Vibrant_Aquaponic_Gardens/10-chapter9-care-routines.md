# Chapter 9: Daily, Weekly, and Monthly Care Routines

Congratulations! Your guppy garden is assembled, cycled, and populated with happy fish and growing plants. Like any garden or aquarium, your aquaponic system will require regular attention to keep it thriving. Establishing consistent care routines is key to long-term success and preventing problems before they escalate. This chapter breaks down the essential tasks into daily, weekly, and monthly schedules.

## Daily Checks (5-10 minutes)

These quick daily observations will help you catch any potential issues early.

*   **1. Fish Observation (Behavior, Appetite, Appearance)**:
    *   **Behavior**: Are your guppies swimming actively? Do they seem alert? Any unusual behavior like lethargy, hiding excessively, gasping at the surface, or rubbing against objects (flashing)?
    *   **Appetite**: When you feed them (see below), do they eat eagerly? A sudden loss of appetite can be an early sign of stress or illness.
    *   **Appearance**: Quickly scan for any visible signs of disease (white spots, frayed fins, sores) or injury. Count your fish if you have a small number to ensure everyone is accounted for.
*   **2. Water Level and Flow**:
    *   **Fish Tank Level**: Check that the water level in the fish tank is appropriate. It will decrease slightly due to evaporation and plant uptake.
    *   **Grow Bed Cycle**: If you have a flood-and-drain media bed with a bell siphon, ensure it's cycling correctly (filling and draining). Check that water is flowing from the pump to the grow bed and draining back to the fish tank smoothly.
    *   **Pump Operation**: Listen to your water pump. Does it sound normal? Is the flow rate adequate?
*   **3. Plant Observation (Wilting, Discoloration, Pests)**:
    *   Quickly inspect your plants. Do they look healthy and turgid?
    *   Any signs of wilting, yellowing leaves, spots, or curling leaves?
    *   Check for any visible pests on the leaves or stems. Early detection is crucial for organic pest management.
*   **4. Temperature Check**:
    *   Glance at your aquarium thermometer to ensure the water temperature in the fish tank is within the ideal range for your guppies (typically 72-79°F or 22-26°C).
*   **5. Feeding Your Guppies**:
    *   **Frequency**: Feed your guppies once or twice a day.
    *   **Amount**: Offer only what they can completely consume within 2-5 minutes. Overfeeding is a common mistake that leads to excess waste, poor water quality, and can harm your fish.
    *   **Food Type**: Use a high-quality flake food or micro-pellets formulated for tropical fish. You can occasionally offer treats like daphnia, brine shrimp (live, frozen, or freeze-dried), or blanched zucchini pieces, but these should be supplementary.
    *   **Observation during feeding**: This is a great time to observe all your fish and ensure everyone is eating.
    *   **Remove Uneaten Food**: If there's significant uneaten food after 5 minutes, remove it with a net to prevent it from decomposing and fouling the water.

## Weekly Tasks (15-30 minutes)

These tasks involve a bit more hands-on attention and are crucial for monitoring the chemical balance of your system.

*   **1. Water Parameter Testing**: This is arguably the most important weekly task.
    *   **Parameters to Test**:
        *   **pH**: Aim for 6.0 - 7.0.
        *   **Ammonia (NH₃/NH₄⁺)**: Should be 0 ppm in a cycled system.
        *   **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)**: Should be 0 ppm in a cycled system.
        *   **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)**: This will be present and is plant food. Levels can range, but typically 5-150 ppm is acceptable. Very high levels (e.g., over 150-200 ppm for extended periods) might eventually stress fish, though plants will consume it.
    *   **Test Kits**: Use reliable liquid test kits (e.g., API Freshwater Master Test Kit). Follow the instructions carefully.
    *   **Record Keeping**: Keep a log of your test results. This helps you track trends and understand your system's natural fluctuations.
*   **2. Topping Off Water (Water Top-Up)**:
    *   Water will be lost through evaporation and plant transpiration.
    *   Top off the fish tank with **dechlorinated water** to maintain the desired level.
    *   **Dechlorination**: Tap water often contains chlorine or chloramine, which are harmful to fish and beneficial bacteria.
        *   Let tap water sit in an open container for 24-48 hours to allow chlorine to dissipate (chloramine will not dissipate this way).
        *   Use a water conditioner/dechlorinator product from a pet store (follow product instructions). This is the safest and quickest method, especially if your water contains chloramine.
*   **3. Basic System Cleaning**:
    *   Wipe down the inside of the fish tank glass if algae or biofilm are building up (an aquarium-safe algae scrubber works well).
    *   Check the water pump intake for any debris (plant matter, etc.) and clean if necessary to ensure good flow.
    *   Visually inspect plumbing for any slow drips or issues.
*   **4. Plant Maintenance**:
    *   **Pruning**: Remove any dead, yellowing, or diseased leaves from your plants. This improves airflow and prevents decay in the system.
    *   **Harvesting**: Harvest outer leaves of leafy greens (cut-and-come-again method) or mature herbs. Regular harvesting encourages new growth.
    *   **Check for Pests**: A more thorough inspection for pests.

## Monthly Tasks (30-60 minutes)

These tasks involve slightly deeper maintenance to keep your system running smoothly long-term.

*   **1. More Thorough Cleaning (As Needed)**:
    *   **Siphoning Solids (Media Beds - Infrequent)**: In a well-balanced media bed system with an appropriate fish load, solid waste often breaks down effectively within the media. However, over time, some solids might accumulate at the bottom of the fish tank or even in the grow bed.
        *   If you notice significant solids buildup in the fish tank, you can use an aquarium siphon/gravel vacuum to gently remove some of it. Be careful not to remove too much water at once.
        *   For media beds, if flow seems impeded or you suspect solids buildup after a long time (e.g., 6-12 months or more), you might *gently* stir a small section of the media or, in rare cases, remove and rinse a portion of the media (using system water to preserve bacteria). This is usually not a frequent requirement.
    *   **Clean Pump**: Remove the water pump and clean its impeller and housing according to the manufacturer's instructions. Mineral deposits or debris can reduce its efficiency.
*   **2. Check and Clean Plumbing**:
    *   Inspect all pipes and tubing for any blockages or buildup. Clean as needed.
    *   If you have a bell siphon, ensure its components are clean and not obstructed.
*   **3. Test Source Water (Occasionally)**:
    *   It's a good idea to occasionally test your tap water (or whatever source water you use for top-offs) for pH and potentially KH (carbonate hardness) and GH (general hardness). This helps you understand if your source water is contributing to any long-term drifts in your system's parameters.
*   **4. Review Your Logbook**:
    *   Look back at your weekly water test results and notes. Are there any concerning trends? Is the system stable? This review can help you anticipate future needs or adjustments.

**Important Considerations**:
*   **Consistency is Key**: Performing these routines regularly is more effective than sporadic, intensive cleaning.
*   **Observe, Observe, Observe**: Your best tool is careful observation. Learn what "normal" looks like for your fish, plants, and system, so you can quickly identify when something is amiss.
*   **Small, Gradual Changes**: If you need to make adjustments (e.g., to pH or water level), do so slowly and gradually to avoid shocking the system.
*   **Don't Over-Clean**: Aquaponics relies on beneficial bacteria. Avoid sterilizing components or doing massive, disruptive cleanings unless absolutely necessary to address a specific problem.

By following these routines, you'll be well on your way to maintaining a healthy, balanced, and vibrant guppy garden that provides you with joy and fresh produce!
