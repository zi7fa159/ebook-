# Appendix B: Supplier List (General Guide)

Finding the components for your guppy garden can be part of the fun! While specific store availability varies greatly by location, this appendix provides a general guide to the types of places where you can typically source the materials and livestock needed for your aquaponic system.

## Fish and Aquarium Supplies

*   **Local Fish Stores (LFS) / Pet Stores**:
    *   **What to find**: Guppies (various varieties), other small community fish (for future consideration), fish food, aquarium tanks (glass and sometimes acrylic), submersible water pumps, air pumps, airline tubing, air stones, aquarium heaters, water conditioners (dechlorinators), water test kits (API Master Test Kit, GH/KH kits), fish nets, algae scrubbers, thermometers, basic aquarium decorations.
    *   **Tips**: Look for stores with healthy, active fish and clean tanks. Staff knowledge can vary.
*   **Online Aquarium Supply Retailers**:
    *   **What to find**: A vast selection of everything listed above, often with more variety and potentially better prices, especially for specialized equipment. Also, live fish can be shipped (ensure reputable sellers with good reviews and live arrival guarantees).
    *   **Tips**: Read reviews carefully, check shipping costs and policies for live animals/plants.
*   **Local Aquarium Clubs or Breeders**:
    *   **What to find**: Often the best source for healthy, locally-bred guppies. Members may also sell or trade used equipment or plants.
    *   **Tips**: A great way to get advice and high-quality livestock.

## Grow Beds, Grow Media, and Plumbing

*   **Hardware Stores / Home Improvement Centers (e.g., Home Depot, Lowe's)**:
    *   **What to find**: Food-grade plastic totes or containers (check recycling codes #2 HDPE, #5 PP for food safety if not explicitly labeled), PVC pipes (various diameters), PVC fittings (elbows, tees, caps, adapters), vinyl tubing, tools (drills, saws, pipe cutters), silicone sealant (look for "aquarium safe" or 100% silicone with no mildew inhibitors), support materials for stands (wood, shelving units).
    *   **Tips**: Bring your component list and measurements. Ask staff for help locating food-grade items if unsure.
*   **Hydroponics and Indoor Gardening Stores (Physical or Online)**:
    *   **What to find**: Expanded clay pebbles (LECA), lava rock, perlite, rockwool cubes, net pots, specialized grow bed containers, water pumps, air pumps, bulkhead fittings, timers, grow lights (LED, T5). Often a one-stop-shop for the "grow" side of aquaponics.
    *   **Tips**: Staff are usually knowledgeable about hydroponics and aquaponics.
*   **Garden Centers / Nurseries**:
    *   **What to find**: Seeds, seedlings/plant starts, perlite, vermiculite, sometimes containers that could be adapted. Some larger centers may have pond supplies (pumps, liners that could be used for larger systems).
    *   **Tips**: Ensure any seedlings purchased have their soil *thoroughly* rinsed off before adding to your aquaponic system.
*   **Agricultural Supply Stores / Farm & Fleet Type Stores**:
    *   **What to find**: Larger food-grade stock tanks (for bigger systems), PVC fittings, sometimes pumps and tubing.
*   **Online Marketplaces (Amazon, eBay, etc.)**:
    *   **What to find**: Virtually all components can be found online, from pumps and media to specialized fittings like bell siphon kits.
    *   **Tips**: Compare prices, read seller reviews, and check specifications carefully to ensure items are suitable and safe for aquaponic use (e.g., food-grade plastics, aquarium-safe pumps).

## Seeds and Plants

*   **Local Garden Centers and Nurseries**: (As mentioned above)
*   **Seed Companies (Online or Mail Order)**:
    *   **What to find**: Huge variety of seeds for leafy greens, herbs, and vegetables. Look for untreated, non-GMO seeds if that is your preference.
    *   **Tips**: Many companies specialize in heirloom or organic seeds.
*   **Farmers Markets**:
    *   **What to find**: Locally grown seedlings. Talk to the farmers about their growing practices. Remember to rinse soil off roots.
*   **Fellow Gardeners/Aquaponic Enthusiasts**:
    *   **What to find**: Seed swaps or plant exchanges can be a great way to get locally adapted varieties for free or low cost.

**Tips for Sourcing Components**:
*   **Prioritize Fish and Plant Safety**: Always opt for food-grade plastics and materials that won't leach harmful chemicals into your system.
*   **Shop Around**: Compare prices, especially for more expensive items like pumps or large quantities of grow media.
*   **Start with a List**: Refer to the components list you made during your planning phase (Chapter 5) to ensure you don't forget anything.
*   **Ask Questions**: Don't hesitate to ask store staff (especially at specialized hydroponics or aquarium stores) for advice on product suitability for aquaponics.

With a bit of searching, you'll find everything you need to build and maintain your thriving guppy garden!
