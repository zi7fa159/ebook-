# Glossary of Aquaponic Terms

Aquaponics, like any specialized field, has its own vocabulary. This glossary defines some of the key terms used throughout "Guppy Gardening: A Beginner's Guide to Vibrant Aquaponic Gardens" to help you navigate the concepts with clarity.

---

**A**

*   **Acclimation**: The process of slowly adjusting fish to new water conditions (temperature, pH, etc.) to minimize stress when moving them to a new tank or system.
*   **Aeration**: The process of dissolving oxygen into the water, typically achieved with an air pump and air stone. Essential for fish, plant roots, and beneficial bacteria.
*   **Alkalinity (Carbonate Hardness, KH)**: A measure of the water's ability to resist changes in pH (its buffering capacity). Primarily composed of carbonates and bicarbonates.
*   **Ammonia (NH₃/NH₄⁺)**: A waste product excreted by fish and produced by the decomposition of organic matter. Highly toxic to fish. It's the first step in the nitrogen cycle.
*   **Aquaculture**: The farming of aquatic organisms such as fish, crustaceans, mollusks, and aquatic plants.
*   **Aquaponics**: A sustainable food production system that combines conventional aquaculture (raising aquatic animals) with hydroponics (cultivating plants in water) in a symbiotic environment. Fish waste provides nutrients for plants, and plants filter the water for the fish.
*   **Auto-Siphon**: See **Bell Siphon**.

**B**

*   **Bacteria (Beneficial)**: Microscopic organisms crucial for the aquaponic nitrogen cycle. Specifically, *Nitrosomonas* (convert ammonia to nitrite) and *Nitrobacter*/*Nitrospira* (convert nitrite to nitrate).
*   **Bell Siphon**: A type of auto-siphon used in media-based grow beds to create a flood-and-drain watering cycle without mechanical timers.
*   **Biofilter / Biofiltration**: A component or process where beneficial bacteria colonize a surface and convert fish waste (ammonia and nitrites) into less harmful nitrates. In media bed systems, the grow media itself acts as a significant biofilter.
*   **Bioload**: The total amount of waste produced by the aquatic animals in a system, which the biofilter must process.
*   **Bolting**: The process where a plant (especially leafy greens or herbs) prematurely sends up a flower stalk and goes to seed, often making the edible leaves bitter. Usually triggered by stress, day length, or age.
*   **Buffering Capacity**: See **Alkalinity**.
*   **Bulkhead Fitting**: A watertight fitting used to create an opening through the wall of a tank or grow bed, typically for a drain.

**C**

*   **Chelated Iron**: A form of iron that is more readily available for plant uptake in aquaponic systems, especially at slightly higher pH levels.
*   **Chloramine**: A disinfectant compound (chlorine + ammonia) used in some municipal water supplies. It is toxic to fish and bacteria and must be neutralized with a water conditioner; it does not dissipate by letting water stand.
*   **Chlorine**: A disinfectant used in tap water, toxic to fish and bacteria. Can be removed by letting water stand for 24-48 hours or instantly with a water conditioner.
*   **Chlorosis**: Yellowing of plant leaves due to a lack of chlorophyll. Interveinal chlorosis means the veins remain green while the tissue between them yellows, often indicating a nutrient deficiency like iron.
*   **Closed-Loop System**: A system where resources (like water and nutrients) are recycled and reused, minimizing waste and external inputs. Aquaponics is a form of closed-loop system.
*   **Cycling**: The process of establishing beneficial nitrifying bacteria in an aquaponic system before adding fish. This converts the system from being unable to process ammonia to being able to convert it efficiently to nitrates.
*   **Cut-and-Come-Again**: A harvesting method for leafy greens and herbs where outer leaves are taken, allowing the plant to continue growing from the center for multiple harvests.

**D**

*   **Dechlorination**: The process of removing chlorine and/or chloramine from tap water before adding it to an aquaponic system.
*   **Deep Water Culture (DWC)**: An aquaponic (or hydroponic) method where plants are suspended on floating rafts with their roots submerged directly in nutrient-rich, aerated water.
*   **Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**: The amount of oxygen gas dissolved in the water, vital for fish, plant roots, and aerobic bacteria.

**F**

*   **Fishless Cycle**: The recommended method of cycling a new aquaponic system by adding an ammonia source (like pure ammonia or fish food) to cultivate bacteria before fish are introduced.
*   **Flashing**: Behavior in fish where they rapidly rub or scratch their bodies against objects in the tank, often a sign of irritation from parasites (like Ich) or poor water quality.
*   **Flood-and-Drain (Ebb and Flow)**: A watering method common in media bed aquaponics where the grow bed is periodically flooded with nutrient-rich water and then allowed to drain, providing both nutrients and oxygen to plant roots. Often achieved with a bell siphon.
*   **Fry**: Baby or juvenile fish.

**G**

*   **General Hardness (GH)**: A measure of the total amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium ions in the water.
*   **Grow Bed**: The container in an aquaponic system where plants are grown.
*   **Grow Lights**: Artificial lights used to provide plants with the necessary light spectrum and intensity for photosynthesis, especially for indoor systems.
*   **Grow Media**: The inert material (e.g., LECA, lava rock) used in media-based grow beds to support plant roots, provide surface area for bacteria, and act as a mechanical filter.
*   **Guppy (Poecilia reticulata)**: A small, hardy, livebearing freshwater fish popular in aquariums and well-suited for beginner aquaponic systems.

**H**

*   **Head Height (Pump)**: The vertical distance a water pump must lift water. Pump flow rates decrease as head height increases.
*   **Hydroponics**: A method of growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions dissolved in water.

**I**

*   **Ich (White Spot Disease - *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*)**: A common parasitic disease affecting fish, characterized by small white spots on the skin and fins.
*   **Inert**: A substance that does not react chemically with its surroundings. Grow media should be inert so it doesn't alter water chemistry.

**K**

*   **KH (Carbonate Hardness)**: See **Alkalinity**.

**L**

*   **LECA (Lightweight Expanded Clay Aggregate)**: A popular, porous, and pH-neutral grow medium for aquaponics and hydroponics. Also known as clay pebbles.
*   **Livebearer**: Fish that give birth to live, free-swimming young (fry), rather than laying eggs. Guppies are livebearers.

**M**

*   **Macronutrients**: Essential plant nutrients required in relatively large amounts (e.g., Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Calcium, Magnesium, Sulfur).
*   **Media Bed**: A type of aquaponic grow bed filled with an inert grow medium.
*   **Micronutrients**: Essential plant nutrients required in very small amounts (e.g., Iron, Manganese, Zinc, Boron).

**N**

*   **NFT (Nutrient Film Technique)**: An aquaponic (or hydroponic) method where plants are grown in channels with their bare roots exposed to a thin, continuous film of nutrient-rich water.
*   **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)**: The end product of the nitrification process, relatively non-toxic to fish and an excellent source of nitrogen for plants.
*   **Nitrification**: The two-step biological process where beneficial bacteria convert ammonia to nitrites, and then nitrites to nitrates.
*   **Nitrifying Bacteria**: The groups of bacteria responsible for nitrification (*Nitrosomonas* and *Nitrobacter*/*Nitrospira*).
*   **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)**: An intermediate product in the nitrogen cycle, formed from the oxidation of ammonia. Highly toxic to fish.
*   **Nitrogen Cycle**: The natural biogeochemical cycle by which nitrogen is converted into various chemical forms as it circulates among the atmosphere, terrestrial, and marine ecosystems. In aquaponics, it specifically refers to the conversion of fish waste (ammonia) into nitrates.
*   **Nutrient Lockout**: A condition where nutrients are present in the system water but plants cannot absorb them, often due to incorrect pH levels.

**O**

*   **Osmoregulation**: The process by which fish control the balance of water and salts in their bodies.

**P**

*   **pH**: A measure of the acidity or alkalinity of water, on a scale of 0-14. A pH of 7 is neutral, below 7 is acidic, and above 7 is alkaline (basic).
*   **PPM (Parts Per Million)**: A unit of concentration often used to measure minerals or chemicals in water (equivalent to mg/L).
*   **Pump (Water Pump)**: A device used to move water from the fish tank to the grow beds.
*   **Pump (Air Pump)**: A device used to pump air into the fish tank, usually through an air stone, to increase dissolved oxygen.

**Q**

*   **Quarantine Tank**: A separate tank used to isolate new fish or treat sick fish to prevent the spread of disease to the main system.

**R**

*   **Recirculating System**: A system where water is continuously reused and cycled, like in aquaponics.
*   **Root Rot**: A condition where plant roots decay, often due to overwatering, poor drainage, or lack of oxygen in the root zone.

**S**

*   **Sump Tank**: An optional, additional tank in an aquaponic system, often placed below the fish tank or grow beds. It can house pumps, heaters, and provide extra water volume and a place for solids to settle.
*   **Symbiosis/Symbiotic**: A close and long-term interaction between two different biological species. In aquaponics, the relationship between fish, bacteria, and plants is symbiotic.

**T**

*   **Transpiration**: The process where plants absorb water through the roots and then give off water vapor through pores in their leaves.

**W**

*   **Water Conditioner / Dechlorinator**: A product used to remove chlorine, chloramine, and sometimes heavy metals from tap water, making it safe for fish and bacteria.
*   **Water Parameters**: The various chemical and physical properties of the water in an aquaponic system (e.g., pH, ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, temperature, GH, KH).

---
This glossary should serve as a helpful reference as you continue your aquaponic endeavors!
