# 06. Lesson 6: Money Matters - Basic Finances for Your Hobby Biz

Understanding and managing your finances is non-negotiable for business success, no matter how small you start. Don't be intimidated; let's cover the basics.

## 6.1 Pricing Your Product/Service

Pricing can be tricky. You need to cover costs, make a profit, and offer value perceived by your customers.
*   **Calculate Your Costs**:
    *   **Direct Costs (Cost of Goods Sold - COGS)**: Materials, supplies directly used to create one unit of your product or deliver your service.
    *   **Time**: How much time does it take you? What's your desired hourly rate (even if you're not paying yourself that yet, it's good to know the value of your labor).
    *   **Indirect Costs (Overheads)**: Website hosting, marketing tools, software, transaction fees, packaging, shipping. (Estimate a portion per item/service).
*   **Research Competitor Pricing**: What are others charging for similar offerings? This gives you a benchmark. Don't just copy; understand their value.
*   **Value-Based Pricing**: What is the perceived value to your customer? Are you offering something unique, high-quality, or highly convenient that warrants a premium price?
*   **Psychological Pricing**: Using prices like $19.99 instead of $20.00.
*   **Profit Margin**: Price = Costs + Profit. Decide on a realistic profit margin.

**Common Mistake**: Underpricing your hobby work because "it's just a hobby." If it's a business, price it like one.

## 6.2 Identifying Your Startup Costs

What do you absolutely *need* to spend money on to get started?
*   **Essentials**: Basic materials/inventory, required licenses/permits, essential tools/software.
*   **Nice-to-Haves**: Professional logo design, fancy packaging, advanced equipment.
*   **Bootstrap Mentality**: Try to start with minimal costs and reinvest profits.
*   **Create a List**: Itemize everything and estimate costs. This will help you understand your initial investment.

## 6.3 Simple Bookkeeping: Tracking Income and Expenses from the Start

You *must* track every penny in and out.
*   **Spreadsheet Method**: A simple Google Sheet or Excel file can work initially. Columns for: Date, Description, Income, Expense, Category (e.g., Materials, Marketing, Fees).
*   **Bookkeeping Software**: As you grow, consider tools like Wave (free), QuickBooks Self-Employed, Xero. These can save time and offer more features.
*   **Keep Receipts**: For all business expenses. Digital copies are great.
*   **Regularly Update**: Don't let it pile up. Set aside time weekly or monthly.

Good records are vital for understanding your business health and for tax time.

## 6.4 Understanding Profit vs. Revenue

*   **Revenue (or Income/Sales)**: The total amount of money your business brings in from sales.
*   **Expenses (or Costs)**: The money your business spends to operate.
*   **Profit (or Net Income)**: Revenue - Expenses. This is what's left over. Profit is the goal!

It's possible to have high revenue but low (or no) profit if your expenses are too high.

## 6.5 When to Consider a Separate Bank Account

*   **Highly Recommended from Day 1**: Even if you're a sole proprietor.
*   **Why?**
    *   **Clarity**: Keeps business and personal finances separate, making bookkeeping much easier.
    *   **Professionalism**: Looks more professional to clients and suppliers.
    *   **Tax Time**: Simplifies identifying business income and deductible expenses.
*   Many banks offer business checking accounts with low or no fees for small businesses.
