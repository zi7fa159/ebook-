# Supporting Characters

## Ben "CreeperKing" Carter
*   **Role**: Obsessed Fan / Online Forum Moderator
*   **Description**: Early 20s, lives and breathes Minecraft lore. Moderates one of the largest online forums dedicated to Minecraft myths and legends, particularly Herobrine. Initially, he's a source of community rumors and compiled "evidence" (screenshots, anecdotal accounts) that Alex dismisses. As events escalate, his forum becomes a crucial hub for sharing information and experiences among affected players.
*   **Motivation**: To be at the center of the Herobrine mystery. He genuinely believes and is both terrified and fascinated. Wants his forum to be the definitive source for all things Herobrine.
*   **Potential Arc**: Starts as somewhat of a caricature of an obsessive fan. However, as the danger becomes real, he might show unexpected bravery or resourcefulness in managing his community and disseminating warnings, becoming a vital communication node for Alex and Eve.

## Agent Davies
*   **Role**: Skeptical Government Agent / Tech Crimes Investigator
*   **Description**: 40s, no-nonsense, by-the-book federal agent, possibly from a cybercrime or domestic terrorism unit. Becomes involved when Herobrine's disruptions start causing real-world issues (e.g., minor infrastructure glitches attributed to server attacks, widespread panic).
*   **Motivation**: To identify the source of the "cyber-attacks" and shut them down. Views the Herobrine explanation as mass hysteria or a cover for malicious human actors.
*   **Potential Arc**: Initially an antagonist to Alex and Eve, viewing them as conspiracy theorists or even perpetrators. Through mounting evidence and perhaps a direct (and deniable) encounter with Herobrine's influence, Davies might slowly and reluctantly begin to consider that there's more to the situation than a standard cybercrime case, leading to a grudging, unofficial cooperation.

## The "Old Timers" (Group)
*   **Role**: Collective of Early Minecraft Players / Keepers of Oral Tradition
*   **Description**: A loose-knit group of individuals who played Minecraft in its earliest alpha and beta stages. They communicate through old, obscure forums or private servers. They possess fragmented memories and anecdotal tales of strange code and unexplained phenomena from the game's nascent period, before Herobrine became a widespread creepypasta.
*   **Motivation**: Some wish to see the "old magic" (or old bugs) of Minecraft acknowledged. Others are wary of digging up the past. They hold pieces of the puzzle regarding Minecraft's foundational code and Mojang's early days that Eve might be trying to reconstruct.
*   **Potential Arc**: They serve as a fragmented chorus, providing cryptic clues and historical context. Some might be helpful, others obstructive. Their collective knowledge, if pieced together, could corroborate Eve's theories or provide a missing link.

## Markus "Notch" Persson (Cameo/Mention)
*   **Role**: Creator of Minecraft / Figure of Myth Himself
*   **Description**: While not an active character, his legacy and past statements (real or fictionalized for the novel) about the game, its code, and perhaps even his own brother (the original Herobrine myth's namesake) could be referenced. Old interviews, blog posts, or even fictionalized journal entries could be "discovered" by the characters.
*   **Motivation (as a story element)**: His "ghost" in the machine serves as a backdrop to the mystery. Was Herobrine an intentional secret, an accidental byproduct, or something else entirely that even the creator didn't fully understand?
*   **Potential Arc**: His past actions or creations become crucial plot points without him needing to be physically present in the main narrative. The characters might be trying to understand his original vision or any safeguards he might have secretly implemented.

## The Affected Players (Group)
*   **Role**: Victims / Witnesses / Source of Escalating Threat
*   **Description**: A growing number of Minecraft players from around the world who begin experiencing Herobrine manifestations. Their accounts, initially dismissed, become too numerous and consistent to ignore. They might experience in-game sabotage, unsettling messages, or even minor real-world electronic disturbances connected to their gameplay.
*   **Motivation**: Fear, confusion, a desire for answers and for the disruptions to stop.
*   **Potential Arc**: Their collective experiences build the sense of scale and urgency for the main characters. Some might fall into paranoia, others might try to fight back in their own ways, creating more chaos or providing useful data. Their plight humanizes the threat beyond just Alex's personal experience.
