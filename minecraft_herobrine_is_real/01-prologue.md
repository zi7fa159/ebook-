# Prologue: The Seed of Doubt

The flickering glow of the monitor painted Alex’s face in hues of blue and grey, the only light in the otherwise obsidian black of his small apartment. It was 3 AM, a time when the digital world often felt more real than the sleeping city outside his window. Tonight, that feeling was particularly acute. He wasn’t just playing Minecraft; he was hunting a ghost.

The server was old, one of those forgotten corners of the internet that had somehow resisted the relentless march of updates and resets. It ran on a near-vanilla version of Alpha, a relic of a bygone era. Rumor had it, this was where *He* was most often sighted. Herobrine. The name itself was a meme, a campfire story for gamers, a creepypasta legend. Alex, a coder by trade and a skeptic by nature, had always dismissed it as such. Pixels couldn't be haunted. Code couldn't harbor malice.

Yet, the stories persisted, evolving from simple, eerie encounters – leafless trees, perfectly cut pyramids in the middle of nowhere, tunnels that seemed too man-made – to more disturbing tales of players being stalked, their worlds subtly altered, their digital lives invaded by an unseen, intelligent presence. Most were easily debunked: glitches, mischievous admins, or simply overactive imaginations.

But a recent string of encounters, documented on an obscure forum Alex frequented, had been different. They were too consistent, too specific, and reported by users known for their level-headedness. The latest post, the one that had dragged Alex down this rabbit hole, included a short video clip. Grainy, low-resolution, it showed a player exploring a newly generated world. In the distance, almost swallowed by the fog, a default Steve skin stood motionless, watching. When the player turned back, it was gone. Standard Herobrine fare, except for one detail: the entity had no nametag, and it wasn't listed among the server's active players.

Alex had replayed the clip a dozen times, analyzing frame by frame. There were no discernible signs of editing. The server IP was shared, and Alex, armed with a fresh install of the correct Minecraft version, had logged in.

For hours, he’d wandered through blocky landscapes, the silence broken only by the rhythmic *thunk* of his pickaxe or the distant moan of a zombie. Nothing. He was about to log off, chalking it up to another wild goose chase, when he saw it.

A perfectly straight 2x2 tunnel, cutting directly into the side of a mountain. It wasn't natural. Torches, crudely placed, lined its interior. Alex’s heart gave an unwelcome flutter. He hadn’t placed them. He was, according to the server list, the only one online.

He hesitated, mouse hovering over the disconnect button. This was stupid. It was a game. But the seed of doubt, once planted, had taken root. He equipped his stone sword, its pixelated edge offering little comfort, and stepped into the darkness. The tunnel was long, descending deeper than seemed possible for a random geological feature. The air in his headphones, usually filled with the game’s ambient sounds, seemed to crackle with a faint, almost inaudible static.

Then, the torches behind him began to extinguish, one by one, plunging the passage into an oppressive blackness.

Alex spun around, his character’s movements jerky and panicked. He fumbled to place a torch from his inventory, its sudden light casting long, dancing shadows. The tunnel behind him was empty, but the oppressive feeling of being watched intensified.

He turned back, deeper into the mountain, a strange compulsion pulling him forward. The tunnel opened into a small cavern. In the center, a single block of Netherrack was set into the stone floor, burning with an eternal flame. And standing just beyond it, almost a silhouette against the dim light, was a figure. Standard Steve skin. Blank, white eyes.

Alex’s breath caught in his throat. His character was frozen. He tried to move, to type, to access the menu, but nothing responded. The figure didn’t move. It just… watched.

Then, a single line of text appeared in his chat window, stark white against the dark interface:

`You shouldn't have come looking.`

The connection to the server dropped.

Alex ripped off his headphones, his heart pounding. His screen showed the familiar Minecraft main menu. He quickly checked the server list. The old Alpha server was gone. Not just offline, but vanished from the list entirely, as if it had never existed. His hands were trembling.

It was just a game, he told himself. A clever prank. A hacked client. There had to be a logical explanation.

But as he stared at his own reflection in the darkened monitor, a new, chilling thought began to form. What if the legends were true? What if Herobrine wasn't just a story?

And what if looking for him was the biggest mistake he’d ever made?

The seed of doubt had blossomed into a cold flower of dread. This was only the beginning.
