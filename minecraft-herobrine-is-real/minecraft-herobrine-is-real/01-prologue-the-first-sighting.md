# Prologue: The First Sighting

The fog was unnatural.

Not the usual, pixelated haze that Minecraft’s render distance sometimes threw up at the edge of vision, but a dense, rolling bank of grey that swallowed chunks whole, silencing the familiar chirps of unseen birds and the rustle of square leaves. Liam, known online as “LiamCrafts,” squinted at his monitor, pushing his glasses further up his nose. He’d been playing on their private server, “Blockhaven,” for hours, meticulously constructing a mountaintop observatory.

“Guys, you seeing this weather?” he typed into the group chat. Alex (“AlphaAlex”), Ben (“LoreMasterBen”), and Chloe (“CodeWitchChloe”) were supposed to be online, gathering resources for the communal town hall far below.

A moment later, AlphaAlex’s reply popped up: *Clear skies down here. You sure your client isn’t bugging out?*

Liam frowned. He toggled the F3 debug screen. No errors. FPS stable. His connection was solid. He typed back, *No, it’s… weird. Like, super thick fog just around my mountain. Can barely see ten blocks ahead.*

He turned his character, Steve, in a slow circle. The half-finished obsidian dome of his observatory loomed, but beyond its perimeter, the world vanished into an oppressive, silent grey. The usual ambient sounds of the game – the distant moan of a zombie, the clucking of a chicken – were gone. It was an eerie, digital silence that prickled the back of his neck.

Then he saw it. Or, *him*.

A figure, standing at the very edge of the fog, just where the render distance should have been painting distant, blocky mountains. Standard Steve skin, just like his own, but… still. Utterly motionless. And its eyes. Even from this distance, they seemed wrong. Blank. White.

Liam’s breath hitched. He wasn’t one for pranks, and neither were his friends, not usually. And this wasn’t a player model he recognized from their small, whitelisted server.

*Someone’s up here with me,* he typed, his fingers fumbling on the keys. *Standard skin. Just… watching.*

LoreMasterBen was next to reply: *Dude. Don’t even joke. Is it… him?*

Liam knew who “him” was. They all did. Herobrine. The ghost in the machine. The campfire story of Minecraft, whispered about in forums and late-night Discord calls. A myth. A hoax.

*Can’t be,* AlphaAlex shot back. *That’s just a story, Ben. Liam, take a screenshot.*

Liam’s hand reached for the F2 key, but as his finger hovered, the figure moved. It didn’t walk. It simply… shifted. One moment it was at the edge of the fog, the next it was closer, impossibly so, as if it had teleported in the blink of an eye that Liam hadn’t actually blinked.

The white eyes were clearer now. Unmistakable.

A cold dread, completely disproportionate to a video game encounter, washed over Liam. This wasn’t a player. This wasn’t a glitch.

He slammed the F2 key.

The figure vanished. The fog remained, thick and cloying, but the watcher was gone.

*Screenshot taken,* Liam typed, his voice a little shaky even in text. *He’s gone now. But he was there. White eyes and everything.*

CodeWitchChloe, ever the pragmatist, finally chimed in: *Server logs show no other player logins, Liam. No unrecognized IPs. Could be a texture pack issue? Or a new mob we don’t know about from a silent update? Highly unlikely, but possible.*

Liam stared at the spot where the figure had been. The silence of the game pressed in. He knew what he saw. And it wasn't a texture pack.

He saved the screenshot, a knot of unease tightening in his stomach. He had a feeling this was more than just a weird bug. This was the start of something.

The fog didn’t lift for another ten minutes. When it finally did, revealing the familiar, sun-drenched landscape of Blockhaven, Liam couldn’t shake the feeling of being watched. He quickly descended the mountain, the half-finished observatory forgotten.

Later that night, after logging off, Liam opened the screenshot. It was… normal. Just his character looking out into the dense fog. No other figure. No white eyes.

He zoomed in, pixel by pixel. Nothing.

It was as if the game itself had decided to hide the evidence. Or as if he’d imagined it.

But Liam knew. He’d seen Herobrine. And a shiver went down his spine that had nothing to do with the late-night chill in his room. The legend felt a little too real tonight.
