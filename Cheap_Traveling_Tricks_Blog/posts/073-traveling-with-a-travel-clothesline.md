## Ditch the Laundromat Fees: The Magic of a Travel Clothesline

For budget travelers, especially those packing light and aiming for carry-on only, doing laundry on the road is a necessity. While laundromats or hostel laundry services are options, they can add up in cost and time. A simple, inexpensive travel clothesline is a fantastic cheap travel trick that allows you to wash small items in your room, saving money and giving you more flexibility.

### Why Pack a Travel Clothesline?

1.  **Save Money on Laundry:**
    *   Avoid paying per load at a laundromat or per item/kilo for hostel laundry services. These costs can accumulate quickly on a long trip.
    *   Washing a few items (underwear, socks, t-shirts) in your sink is practically free.
2.  **Convenience:**
    *   Do laundry on your own schedule, without having to find a laundromat or wait for a machine to be free.
    *   Perfect for quickly washing an item you need for the next day.
3.  **Pack Lighter:**
    *   Knowing you can easily wash clothes means you can pack fewer items, helping you achieve that minimalist carry-on goal.
4.  **Handle Unexpected Spills or Wet Gear:**
    *   Quickly dry a rain-soaked shirt or a swimsuit without having to run a full laundry load.
5.  **Versatility:**
    *   Can be strung up in hostel rooms (between bunks, if allowed and respectful), bathrooms, balconies, or even outdoors between trees if camping.

### Types of Travel Clotheslines:

*   **Twisted Elastic/Bungee Cord Style:**
    *   **How it Works:** These lines are made of two or more strands of elastic cord twisted together. You tuck the corners of your clothes between the twists – no clothespins needed!
    *   **Attachment:** Usually have Velcro straps, suction cups, or hooks/carabiners at each end for easy attachment to various anchor points.
    *   **Pros:** Lightweight, compact, no clothespins required (which can get lost).
    *   **Cons:** Might not hold heavier items as securely as lines with pegs. Suction cups may not stick to all surfaces.
    *   **Examples:** Sea to Summit Lite Line Clothesline, Rick Steves Travel Clothesline.
*   **Pegless Clotheslines with Beads/Stops:**
    *   **How it Works:** A single cord with small beads or plastic stops along its length. You weave your clothing around these stops to secure them.
    *   **Pros:** Can be very lightweight.
    *   **Cons:** May hold fewer items or be less secure for heavier garments than twisted lines or lines with pegs.
*   **Clotheslines with Built-in or Separate Clothespins:**
    *   **How it Works:** A simple cord with small, lightweight clothespins.
    *   **Pros:** Can offer a stronger grip for heavier or more delicate items.
    *   **Cons:** Clothespins can add a tiny bit of bulk or get lost if separate.
*   **DIY Clothesline:**
    *   A length of strong, thin cord (e.g., paracord, nylon string) and a few safety pins or binder clips can work in a pinch. Less convenient but very cheap.

### What to Look for in a Good Travel Clothesline:

*   **Lightweight and Compact:** Essential for travel.
*   **Durable Material:** Should withstand repeated use and some tension.
*   **Easy to Attach:** Versatile attachment options (hooks, Velcro, suction cups) are a plus.
*   **Sufficient Length:** Around 6-10 feet (2-3 meters) is usually adequate for a few small items.
*   **Good Grip (if pegless):** The twists or beads should hold clothes securely.

### How to Use Your Travel Clothesline Effectively:

1.  **Choose Your Location Wisely:**
    *   **Bathroom:** Often the best option due to ventilation (if there's a fan or window) and tiled surfaces for suction cups. Shower rods or towel bars can be anchor points.
    *   **Balcony (if available and permitted):** Offers good airflow.
    *   **Hostel Bunk:** Be considerate of roommates. Only use your bunk space and ensure it's not dripping on their belongings.
    *   **Outdoors (Camping):** Between trees or poles.
2.  **Secure Both Ends Tightly:** Pull the line taut for best results, but not so tight that you damage the anchor points or the line itself.
3.  **Wring Out Clothes Thoroughly:** The less water in the clothes, the faster they'll dry and the less strain on the line.
    *   **Travel Towel Trick:** After hand washing, lay the wet item flat on a quick-dry travel towel, roll them up tightly together, and wring. The towel will absorb a lot of excess water.
4.  **Distribute Weight Evenly:** Hang heavier items closer to the ends/anchor points if possible.
5.  **Maximize Airflow:** Don't overcrowd the line. Leave space between items for air to circulate.
6.  **Turn Items Periodically:** If drying indoors, flipping items can help them dry more evenly.
7.  **Be Patient:** Air drying takes time, especially in humid climates or poorly ventilated rooms. Plan accordingly.
8.  **Consider a Quick-Drying Fabric Soap:** Some travel laundry soaps are designed to rinse out easily and are suitable for quick-drying fabrics.

### What to Wash on Your Travel Clothesline:

Best suited for:
*   Underwear
*   Socks
*   Lightweight T-shirts or tank tops
*   Swimwear
*   Small, quick-drying items

Heavier items like jeans or thick sweaters will take a very long time to air dry and might be better saved for a proper laundromat.

A travel clothesline is a tiny investment (often $5-$15) that can yield significant savings and convenience on the road. It empowers budget travelers to pack lighter, stay fresh, and avoid the dreaded laundromat hunt when all you need is a clean pair of socks. It's a simple, effective cheap travel trick that truly embodies resourceful globetrotting.
