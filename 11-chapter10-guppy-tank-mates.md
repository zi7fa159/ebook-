# Chapter 10: Guppy Tank Mates – Choosing Companions Wisely

While your primary focus might be on breeding guppies, you may wish to keep them in a community setting or alongside other species in a larger display tank. Choosing appropriate tank mates is crucial for the well-being of your guppies, especially if you want to maximize fry survival or prevent stress that could hinder breeding.

## 10.1. General Considerations for Choosing Tank Mates

*   **Temperament:** Guppies are generally peaceful, but they are small and can be easily bullied or outcompeted for food by larger or more aggressive fish. Tank mates should also be peaceful.
*   **Size:** Avoid fish that are significantly larger than adult guppies, as they might see guppies (especially males with flowing fins) as food. Conversely, very tiny tank mates might be harassed by boisterous guppies.
*   **Water Parameter Compatibility:** All tank mates must thrive in the same water parameters (temperature, pH, GH, KH) that your guppies prefer (tropical, slightly alkaline, moderately hard water).
*   **Fin-Nipping:** Male guppies' long, flowing fins can be irresistible targets for notorious fin-nipping species. Avoid these fish at all costs.
*   **Predation on Fry:** Most fish will eat guppy fry if given the chance. If fry survival is a goal in a community tank, this is a major consideration. Extremely dense plant cover is essential.
*   **Activity Level and Swimming Zone:** Consider if the tank mates occupy different levels of the aquarium (top, middle, bottom dwellers) to reduce direct competition for space.
*   **Dietary Needs:** Ensure all tank mates have compatible dietary requirements.

## 10.2. Good Tank Mate Choices for Guppies

These species are generally considered peaceful and compatible with guppies, though individual fish personalities can vary.

*   **Other Small Livebearers:**
    *   **Endler's Livebearers (*Poecilia wingei*):** Very similar to guppies in care and temperament. They can interbreed with guppies, producing fertile hybrids. If you want to keep pure guppy strains, do not house them with Endler's (unless that's your specific breeding project).
    *   **Platies (*Xiphophorus maculatus* / *variatus*):** Peaceful, colorful, and generally good community fish. They are larger than guppies but usually not aggressive towards them. They will readily eat fry.
    *   **Mollies (*Poecilia sphenops*, *latipinna*, *velifera*):** Generally peaceful, but some, like sailfin mollies, can get quite large and may require bigger tanks. Short-fin varieties are usually fine. They also prefer slightly harder, more alkaline water, often with a touch of salt (though guppies also tolerate this). Will eat fry.
*   **Small, Peaceful Tetras:**
    *   **Neon Tetras (*Paracheirodon innesi*):** Peaceful, schooling fish. Keep in groups of 6+.
    *   **Cardinal Tetras (*Paracheirodon axelrodi*):** Similar to neons but slightly larger and with a full red stripe.
    *   **Glowlight Tetras (*Hemigrammus erythrozonus*):** Peaceful and hardy.
    *   **Ember Tetras (*Hyphessobrycon amandae*):** Very small and peaceful, good for smaller tanks.
    *   **Harlequin Rasboras (*Trigonostigma heteromorpha*):** Peaceful, schooling fish that add nice activity.
    *   *Avoid larger, more boisterous tetras like serpae tetras or black skirt tetras, which can be fin-nippers.*
*   **Corydoras Catfish (Corys):**
    *   Excellent bottom dwellers. Peaceful, social (keep in groups of 3-6+ of their own species), and help clean up leftover food.
    *   Many varieties exist (e.g., Panda Cory, Bronze Cory, Albino Cory, Pygmy Cory). Pygmy Corys (*Corydoras pygmaeus*) are particularly good for smaller tanks as they stay very small.
    *   They are generally not a threat to adult guppies but might opportunistically eat very young fry that venture to the bottom.
*   **Otocinclus Catfish (Otos):**
    *   Small, peaceful algae eaters. Best kept in small groups. They are sensitive to water quality and need a mature tank with some algae growth or supplemented algae wafers.
*   **Bristlenose Plecos (*Ancistrus* sp.):**
    *   One of the smaller and more manageable pleco species (typically grow to 4-6 inches). Good algae eaters.
    *   Ensure the tank is large enough (20-30 gallons minimum for an adult). They produce a fair amount of waste.
    *   *Avoid common plecos (*Pterygoplichthys gibbiceps*), which grow very large.*
*   **Small, Peaceful Rasboras:**
    *   **Chili Rasboras (*Boraras brigittae*):** Tiny, colorful, and peaceful. Ideal for nano tanks or with smaller guppy strains.
    *   **Celestial Pearl Danios (Galaxy Rasboras) (*Danio margaritatus*):** Beautiful, small, and generally peaceful, though sometimes a bit shy.
*   **Freshwater Snails:**
    *   **Nerite Snails:** Excellent algae eaters, won't reproduce in freshwater (eggs won't hatch). Many patterns available.
    *   **Mystery Snails:** Larger, peaceful snails. Can lay clutches of eggs above the waterline, which can be removed if you don't want baby snails.
    *   **Ramshorn Snails / Bladder Snails:** Often appear as hitchhikers. Can reproduce quickly and become pests if overfed, but also contribute to the cleanup crew.
*   **Freshwater Shrimp:**
    *   **Amano Shrimp:** Larger, clear shrimp, good algae eaters, generally peaceful.
    *   **Cherry Shrimp (and other *Neocaridina davidi* varieties):** Small, colorful shrimp. They can be good tank mates, but adult guppies *may* prey on baby shrimp (shrimplets). If you want to breed shrimp, a species-only tank is best. Dense moss and hiding spots are essential for shrimplet survival with guppies.
    *   **Ghost Shrimp:** Inexpensive, mostly translucent. Can be good cleaners, but some larger individuals might occasionally nip at flowing fins if not well-fed.

## 10.3. Tank Mates to AVOID with Guppies

*   **Aggressive or Semi-Aggressive Fish:**
    *   **Most Cichlids:** (e.g., Angelfish (can be okay when young, but risky with adult guppies' fins), Convict Cichlids, Jack Dempseys, Oscars). They will bully, injure, or eat guppies. Some dwarf cichlids like Rams or Apistogrammas *might* work with caution in larger, well-planted tanks, but there's always a risk.
    *   **Betta Fish (Siamese Fighting Fish):** While sometimes kept together, it's risky. Male bettas may see male guppies' colorful fins as a challenge and attack them. Female bettas can also be aggressive. If attempted, it needs a large tank with many hiding spots and careful observation.
    *   **Gouramis (Larger Varieties):** Species like Blue, Gold, or Opaline Gouramis can become territorial and aggressive towards smaller fish like guppies. Dwarf Gouramis (*Trichogaster lalius*) are generally more peaceful but can still be hit-or-miss.
*   **Known Fin-Nippers:**
    *   **Tiger Barbs:** Notorious fin-nippers, especially when not kept in large enough schools.
    *   **Serpae Tetras:** Can be very nippy.
    *   **Black Skirt Tetras (Black Widow Tetras):** Can also be nippy.
    *   Some larger Danios if not in a proper school.
*   **Large Predatory Fish:**
    *   Any fish large enough to fit a guppy in its mouth will likely eat it. This includes many larger catfish, predatory barbs, etc.
*   **Goldfish:**
    *   Goldfish are cold-water fish and have different temperature and care requirements than tropical guppies. They also get very large and produce a lot of waste. Not compatible.
*   **Crayfish / Crabs (most types):**
    *   Opportunistic predators that can catch and eat sleeping or slow-moving fish. Dwarf Mexican Crayfish (*Cambarellus patzcuarensis*) might be an exception if well-fed and in a large enough tank with hiding spots, but still a risk.

## 10.4. Considerations for a Guppy-Only Breeding Tank

If your primary goal is to breed a specific guppy strain or maximize fry production, a species-only tank (only guppies) is often the best approach.

*   **Advantages:**
    *   No threat from other species.
    *   Easier to control water parameters specifically for guppies.
    *   Easier to manage fry (though adult guppies will still eat fry if not enough cover is provided).
    *   Simpler feeding regimen.
*   **Setup:**
    *   Can be a dedicated tank for a breeding colony (e.g., several males and multiple females).
    *   Or, separate tanks for specific breeding pairs/trios, and separate grow-out tanks for fry.

When choosing tank mates, research each species thoroughly. What works in one person's tank might not work in another's due to differences in tank size, setup, and individual fish personalities. Always prioritize the health and safety of your guppies. Observation is key after introducing any new tank mate. Be prepared to separate fish if you see signs of aggression or stress.
