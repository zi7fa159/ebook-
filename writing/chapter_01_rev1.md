**Chapter 1: The Gas Leak**

The migraine was a creature. It had been living behind Leo Gallagher’s eyes for a month, a coiled thing that tightened its grip whenever he stared at a screen too long. Normal headaches didn’t hum. They didn’t fill the quiet moments with a low, staticky buzz, like a radio searching for a signal that wasn't there.

It was this creature, this static, that had driven him to it. In a fit of caffeine-fueled desperation at three in the morning, he’d done something profoundly stupid. He’d hacked the SCP Foundation. Well, "hacked" was a generous term. He’d found a vulnerability, a single loose thread in the impossibly complex tapestry of their intranet, and he’d pulled it. It led him to a low-level, poorly-secured medical diagnostic server. With his heart hammering a frantic rhythm against his ribs—a frantic counterpoint to the steady hum in his skull—he typed his symptoms into the sterile interface. *Chronic migraine. Auditory hallucinations.*

The result wasn't a diagnosis. It was a designation. **SCP-XXXX-B.**

For forty-eight hours, the silence that followed was louder than the static had ever been. He’d expected a bang. A black helicopter, MTF operators, the whole nine yards. He’d even rehearsed a one-liner.

Instead, he got a polite knock.

He opened the door to two men in gray jumpsuits reading "Sanitary & Custodial Professionals." The older one looked monumentally unimpressed with the universe.

“Mr. Gallagher?” he said, his voice flat. “Reports of a gas leak. We need to evacuate the floor.”

This was it? The legendary Veil protocol? Not a show of force, but a show of utter, soul-crushing banality.

“A gas leak? I don’t smell anything,” Leo said, the witty retort he’d practiced dying on his tongue.

“That’s the dangerous thing about these new synthetics, sir. Odorless,” the younger one said, a little too cheerfully. “Standard procedure. We’re offering complimentary bottled water for the inconvenience.”

Leo saw the bottle and almost laughed. The oldest trick in the book. He, a lore master, was not about to fall for it. Except he hadn’t slept, his apartment was a mess, and the creature behind his eyes was squeezing again. He took the bottle.

“Well, if it’s standard procedure,” he said, a final, defiant spark of his online persona flaring up. He took a long swig. It was just water. Cold, crisp, disappointingly normal water.

His next thought was that the scuffed linoleum of his hallway floor was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen. He decided to lie down and appreciate it.

He awoke to a silence so profound it felt like a sound. The room was aggressively, unnervingly white. White bed, white sheets, white walls. A mirrorless bathroom to his left, a desk to his right. In front of him, a seamless gray wall.

“One-way observation window,” Leo croaked, his throat dry. A ridiculous thrill shot through him. He was *in*. This was the real deal. He walked to the gray wall and knocked. “Hello in there! The complimentary water service was a little heavy-handed, 0/10, would not recommend.”

A speaker in the ceiling crackled. A calm, female voice. “SCP-XXXX-A, please return to your bed. A medical professional will be with you shortly.”

Leo grinned, a wide, manic smile. *SCP-XXXX-A*. He had a designation. He was canon. The fear was there, a cold knot in his stomach, but the sheer, absurd novelty of it all was intoxicating.

“Actually, it’s Leo,” he called out. “And technically, I’m the host for SCP-XXXX-B. Let’s try to use proper nomenclature, people. It’s in the guidelines.”

The only reply was the humming silence. For the first time, Leo noticed it wasn’t just in his head anymore. The whole room was vibrating with it. The static was everywhere.
