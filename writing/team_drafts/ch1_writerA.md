**Chapter 1: The Gas Leak**

Leo Gallagher’s world didn’t end with a bang, but with a polite knock. He’d been expecting a bang. For the last forty-eight hours, ever since he’d leveraged a frankly irresponsible amount of caffeine and a sliver of exposed code to punch a hole in the SCP Foundation’s intranet, he’d been expecting a black helicopter to descend upon his apartment complex. He’d envisioned men in tactical gear, designated "MTF Epsilon-11, Nine-Tailed Fox," kicking down his door. He’d even prepared a witty one-liner. Something about them being slow on the uptake.

Instead, he got two men in matching gray jumpsuits with the logo "Sanitary & Custodial Professionals" embroidered on the breast. The older one had a weary, patient face, the kind of man who’d seen a thousand overflowing toilets and wasn’t impressed by any of them.

“Mr. Gallagher?” the man said, not unkindly. “We’ve had reports of a gas leak in this unit. For your safety, we need to evacuate the floor.”

Leo stared. This was it? This was the legendary Veil protocol? Not a thundering display of covert power, but the sheer, crushing banality of a maintenance call. He almost felt insulted.

“A gas leak? I don’t smell anything,” Leo said, his prepared one-liner dying on his lips.

“That’s the dangerous thing about these new synthetic compounds, sir. Odorless,” the younger man chirped, holding up a clipboard. “Standard procedure. We’ll have you back in a jiffy. We’re offering complimentary bottled water for the inconvenience.”

Leo, an expert in Foundation lore, knew all about the memory-wiping amnestics. He knew they were often administered orally, in a liquid medium. He knew this was the oldest trick in their very thick book. He also knew he hadn’t slept in two days, his apartment smelled like stale pizza, and his head was pounding from the migraine that had started this whole mess. He took the bottle.

“Well, if it’s standard procedure,” he said, twisting the cap with a flourish of ironic bravado. He took a long, defiant swig. It tasted like…water. Perfectly normal, crisp water. Maybe he’d been wrong. Maybe this really was just about a gas leak. It was a disappointing thought.

His second thought, which arrived about three seconds later, was that the floor was suddenly very, very interesting. And soft. He decided to lie down on it to get a closer look.

He awoke in a room that was aggressively white. The walls, the sheets, the seamless ceiling—it was the kind of white that absorbed sound. He sat up. The bed was surprisingly comfortable. To his left was a small, attached bathroom with a toilet and sink. No mirror. Classic. To his right, a simple desk and chair. The wall in front of him was a flat, gray, metallic surface.

“One-way observation window,” Leo said aloud to the empty room, his voice a dry croak. He felt a ridiculous surge of pride. He’d nailed it. He was in a Standard Humanoid Containment Cell. He’d read the blueprints for this exact model on the wiki. He stood up, walked to the gray wall, and knocked on it with his knuckles.

“Hello in there! Is this where I file a complaint with HR? The complimentary water service was, frankly, a little heavy-handed.”

A speaker in the ceiling crackled to life. A woman’s voice, calm and measured, filled the room. “SCP-XXXX-A, please return to your bed. A medical professional will be with you shortly.”

Leo grinned. *SCP-XXXX-A*. He had a number. He was canon. The absurdity of it was intoxicating. He felt less like a prisoner and more like an honored guest at the world’s most exclusive, and dangerous, convention.

“Actually, it’s Leo,” he said to the ceiling. “And I think you’ll find I’m not an ‘A’. I’m the host for SCP-XXXX-B. Let’s try to use proper nomenclature, people. It’s in the guidelines.”

The silence that followed was, for the first time, a little unnerving.
