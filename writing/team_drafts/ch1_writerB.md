**Chapter 1: Static**

The migraine was a creature. It lived behind Leo’s eyes, coiling and uncoiling in time with his pulse. It wasn’t a normal headache. Normal headaches didn’t come with the whispers. They were faint, like voices on a radio caught between stations, a sea of static out of which half-formed words would occasionally surface. For weeks, he’d been telling himself it was just auditory pareidolia, his brain trying to make sense of the random noise of his own blood pressure.

He’d only broken into the Foundation’s network out of desperation. The whispers were getting louder, and he was getting scared. In the deepest, most terrified corner of his mind, he knew this was anomalous. He was a fan, an expert in their fiction, but the fear felt profoundly real. He’d found their diagnostic server and typed in his symptoms, his fingers trembling. *Chronic migraine. Auditory hallucinations. Aphasia.*

The result that flashed on the screen wasn't a diagnosis. It was a designation. **SCP-XXXX-B.**

Now, the silence was louder than the whispers had ever been. He sat in the white room, on the white bed, and tried to regulate his breathing. The quiet was a physical presence, pressing in on him. He felt… hollowed out. He had woken up here after the men with the bottled water had come. He remembered their dead, patient eyes.

He touched his own face. It felt distant, like he was touching someone else’s skin. Was he still Leo? Or was he just the container now? The thought sent a tremor through him. He stood and paced the small room. Ten steps one way, ten steps back. The wall in front of him was a dull gray. He knew what it was. A one-way window. He was a specimen on a slide.

He pressed his ear against the cold metal, listening. Nothing. Just the muffled thrum of his own terrified heart.

“Hello?” he whispered.

A voice from the ceiling made him jolt so hard he stumbled back. “SCP-XXXX-A, please return to your bed. A medical professional will be with you shortly.”

*SCP-XXXX-A.* Not Leo. The designation wasn't a cool badge of honor, as his fanboy-self might have fantasized. It was a stripping away. An erasure. ‘A’ for the vessel. ‘B’ for the thing inside. They’d already categorized him, dissected him with their language.

He backed away from the wall until his back hit the opposite wall and he slid down to the floor. The whispers were starting again, at the edge of his hearing. Or were they inside his head now? He couldn't tell. That was the most terrifying part. The static was coming from inside the house. He squeezed his eyes shut. The creature was uncoiling again.
