# An Introduction to the SCP Foundation: Secure, Contain, Protect

## 1. Unveiling the Veil: What is the SCP Foundation?

The SCP Foundation is a clandestine, global organization operating beyond the reach of any single government or jurisdiction. Its mission, encapsulated by its motto "Secure, Contain, Protect," is to locate, study, and neutralize anomalous objects, entities, locations, and phenomena that defy natural law and pose a potential threat to humanity or its perception of reality. These anomalies are referred to as "SCPs" (Special Containment Procedures or the anomalous item/entity itself).

Imagine a world where the strange and inexplicable are not just figments of imagination but tangible realities. The Foundation exists as humanity's shield against these unknown horrors and wonders, working in the shadows to maintain normalcy and prevent mass hysteria or existential threats. Its operations are vast, its resources seemingly limitless, and its methods often morally ambiguous, driven by a pragmatic utilitarianism that prioritizes the survival of the species above all else.

The Foundation is not a benevolent entity in the traditional sense. While its goal is the protection of humanity, its methods can be ruthless. It employs researchers, security personnel, and specialized task forces, often exposing them to extreme danger. Information is strictly controlled through a hierarchical clearance level system, ensuring that only those who "need to know" have access to sensitive data about specific SCPs or the Foundation's broader operations. This secrecy is paramount, as public knowledge of the anomalies the Foundation contains could lead to global panic, societal collapse, or the weaponization of these dangerous phenomena by rival organizations or rogue states.

The lore of the SCP Foundation is a collaborative fiction project, originating from the /x/ - Paranormal board on 4chan in 2007 with the posting of SCP-173, "The Sculpture." It has since evolved into a massive, community-driven universe with thousands of articles, tales, and canons, all hosted on the SCP Wiki. This collaborative nature means the Foundation's universe is constantly expanding, with diverse interpretations and a rich tapestry of interconnected stories.

## 2. The Core Mission: Secure, Contain, Protect

The Foundation's motto defines its operational imperatives:

### Secure:
This involves the identification, tracking, and initial acquisition of anomalous objects or entities. Foundation agents, often part of Mobile Task Forces (MTFs), are deployed globally to investigate reports of anomalous activity. Once an anomaly is confirmed, protocols are enacted to secure the area, prevent civilian interference, and bring the anomaly under Foundation control. This phase is often dangerous, as the nature and capabilities of new anomalies are typically unknown. Securing an anomaly might involve complex logistics, combat engagements, or intricate deception campaigns to retrieve it without alerting the public. For example, securing a geographically fixed anomaly like an anomalous building might involve purchasing the entire area under a shell corporation and establishing a permanent research site.

### Contain:
Once secured, an SCP is transported to a Foundation facility, where Special Containment Procedures are developed and implemented. These procedures are tailored to the specific nature of each anomaly and are designed to prevent its escape, neutralize its harmful effects, and limit interaction with the outside world. Containment can range from simply locking an object in a box (for Safe-class SCPs) to elaborate, resource-intensive protocols for highly dangerous or reality-bending entities (Keter or Thaumiel-class SCPs). The Foundation maintains numerous secure sites, ranging from small outposts to vast, underground complexes, each designed to house specific types of anomalies. The documentation for each SCP meticulously details these procedures, which are subject to constant review and revision as more is learned about the anomaly.

### Protect:
This is the overarching goal: to protect humanity from the effects of these anomalies and, in some interpretations, to protect the anomalies themselves from being misused or misunderstood. This protection extends to maintaining global normalcy and preventing the general populace from becoming aware of the true, often terrifying, nature of the universe. The Foundation achieves this through widespread information suppression, memory alteration using amnestics, and the creation of cover stories to explain away anomalous events. The "veil" of secrecy is considered crucial for societal stability. Protecting humanity also involves extensive research into the anomalies to understand their properties, origins, and potential applications (though weaponization is generally frowned upon, practical applications for containment or understanding other SCPs are explored).

## 3. Understanding SCP Classification

SCPs are categorized by their containment difficulty, not necessarily their direct threat level, although the two are often correlated. The primary Object Classes are:

*   **Safe:** Anomalies that are well-understood, easily and reliably contained, or whose anomalous effects are only triggered by specific, intentional activation. "Safe" does not mean harmless; a Safe-class SCP could still be incredibly dangerous if its containment procedures are breached or if it's mishandled. For example, a button that destroys the universe when pressed could be Safe if it's kept in a locked box and no one presses it.
*   **Euclid:** Anomalies that require more resources to contain completely or where containment isn't always reliable. Often, this is because the anomaly is not fully understood, behaves unpredictably, or is autonomous. Many sentient SCPs fall into this category. Euclid is the most common classification, representing a broad range of anomalies whose containment requires active monitoring and adjustment.
*   **Keter:** Anomalies that are exceedingly difficult to contain consistently or reliably, with procedures often being extensive and complex. Keter-class SCPs pose a significant threat to Foundation personnel and the general populace if they breach containment. They may be actively hostile, capable of widespread destruction, or possess reality-altering properties that make permanent containment a constant struggle. Containing Keter SCPs often consumes a disproportionate amount of the Foundation's resources.

Beyond these primary classes, there are others:

*   **Thaumiel:** Anomalies that the Foundation uses to contain or counteract other SCPs or anomalous phenomena. Thaumiel-class SCPs are highly classified, and their existence is known to few within the Foundation. They represent some of the Foundation's most powerful assets and often have unique, reality-defining properties.
*   **Neutralized:** Anomalies that have been destroyed, disabled, or no longer exhibit anomalous properties. This can occur through intentional action, accident, or unknown reasons.
*   **Explained:** Anomalies whose properties are eventually understood to be mundane science, debunked, or misidentified, and are no longer considered anomalous.
*   **Apollyon:** A relatively newer class, representing anomalies that are impossible to contain and are expected to cause an XK-class (end-of-the-world) scenario or similar catastrophic event in the near future. These are threats that the Foundation can, at best, only study and prepare for the inevitable.
*   **Archon:** Anomalies that could theoretically be contained but are intentionally left uncontained for various reasons, often because their containment would be more detrimental than allowing them to remain active under specific conditions.

There are also esoteric or narrative-specific classes, but the above represent the most commonly encountered.

## 4. Key Players and Groups of Interest

The SCP Foundation is not the only organization aware of or interacting with anomalies. The SCP universe is populated by various "Groups of Interest" (GoIs), each with its own agenda:

*   **The Global Occult Coalition (GOC):** An agency of the United Nations, the GOC's mission is typically to destroy paranormal threats rather than contain them. They often clash with the Foundation over methodology, viewing anomalies as inherently dangerous perversions of reality that must be eradicated. Their approach is "Destruction, Destruction, Destruction."
*   **The Chaos Insurgency (CI):** A splinter group that defected from the SCP Foundation. Their motives are often portrayed as seeking to use SCPs for their own benefit, whether for personal power, global destabilization, or other nefarious purposes. They are a primary antagonist to the Foundation.
*   **The Serpent's Hand:** A small but highly elusive organization that advocates for the rights and liberation of anomalous individuals and objects. They believe that anomalies should be accepted and understood, not imprisoned or destroyed, and often attempt to free SCPs from Foundation or GOC custody.
*   **Are We Cool Yet? (AWCY?):** An artists' collective that creates anomalous art ("anart"). Their works range from whimsical to horrifying, often with a disregard for the consequences they might cause. Their motivations are often rooted in artistic expression or social commentary, using the anomalous as their medium.
*   **Marshall, Carter, and Dark Ltd. (MC&D):** An exclusive "gentlemen's club" that caters to the ultra-wealthy, procuring and selling anomalous items and experiences. They are driven by profit and exclusivity, viewing anomalies as commodities.
*   **Doctor Wondertainment:** An individual or entity that creates anomalous toys and games, often with dangerous or unpredictable properties. Their products are usually branded with a whimsical, child-friendly aesthetic that belies their potential for harm.

These are just a few examples; the SCP universe is rich with diverse factions, each adding layers of complexity to the Foundation's mission.

## 5. The Foundation's Internal Structure

The Foundation's internal structure is vast and complex, designed for efficiency and secrecy:

*   **The O5 Council (Overseer Council):** The highest authority within the Foundation, typically composed of 13 individuals (O5-1 through O5-13) who have ultimate oversight over all Foundation operations. Their identities are among the Foundation's most closely guarded secrets, and they possess Level 5 clearance. They make the most critical decisions, often involving life-or-death choices for millions.
*   **Site Directors:** Each Foundation facility (Site or Area) is managed by a Site Director, who has considerable autonomy over their specific location and the anomalies contained therein. They report to the O5 Council or their designated liaisons.
*   **Ethics Committee:** An internal body responsible for reviewing the morality and ethical implications of containment procedures and Foundation actions. While often portrayed as having limited real power against the pragmatic needs of containment, they serve as a check on the Foundation's potential excesses.
*   **Mobile Task Forces (MTFs):** Elite, specialized units that are deployed to handle specific threats or situations. Each MTF has a designation (e.g., MTF Epsilon-11 "Nine-Tailed Fox" for internal security and re-containment, or MTF Alpha-1 "Red Right Hand" operating directly under O5 orders). There are hundreds of MTFs, each with unique training and equipment suited to various types of anomalous threats.
*   **Researchers:** The scientific backbone of the Foundation. They study anomalies, develop containment procedures, and strive to understand the underlying principles of anomalous phenomena. They come from diverse scientific backgrounds.
*   **D-Class Personnel:** "Disposable" personnel, typically drawn from death row inmates or political prisoners (though this varies by canon). They are used for handling extremely dangerous SCPs or conducting hazardous experiments where loss of life is considered acceptable. Their use is one of the most controversial aspects of the Foundation's operations.

## 6. Why the SCP Foundation Captivates: Themes and Appeal

The enduring popularity of the SCP Foundation stems from several key themes:

*   **The Allure of the Unknown:** It taps into the primal human fascination with mystery, the unexplained, and things that go bump in the night.
*   **Cosmic Horror:** Many SCPs evoke a sense of Lovecraftian horror – the idea that humanity is insignificant in a universe filled with entities and forces beyond its comprehension and control.
*   **Bureaucracy and the Mundane:** The contrast between terrifying, reality-bending anomalies and the dry, clinical, bureaucratic language used to describe them creates a unique and often darkly humorous tone. The mundane procedures make the extraordinary seem almost routine.
*   **Moral Ambiguity:** The Foundation operates in a grey area. Is it a necessary evil? Are its methods justified? This moral complexity invites discussion and varied interpretations.
*   **Collaborative Creativity:** As a community-driven project, it offers a platform for countless writers to contribute their ideas, creating an incredibly diverse and ever-expanding universe. Anyone can write an SCP or a tale.
*   **Containment Fiction:** The core concept of "monster of the week" combined with intricate lore and world-building provides endless storytelling potential. Each SCP entry is a micro-story, a puzzle box of containment.

## 7. Getting Started with the SCP Universe

For newcomers, the sheer volume of content can be daunting. Here are some starting points:

*   **The SCP Wiki:** The official home of the SCP Foundation (scp-wiki.wikidot.com). Start with some of the classic, highly-rated articles from Series I (SCPs 001-999).
*   **Heritage Collection:** A curated list of foundational and influential SCPs.
*   **Top Rated Pages:** See what the community considers the best of the best.
*   **Canons and Story Hubs:** Explore interconnected narratives and specific storylines within the broader universe.
*   **YouTube Channels and Podcasts:** Many creators produce excellent readings, explanations, and animated content based on SCP lore (e.g., The Volgun, SCP Explained - Story & Animation, The Exploring Series).

Be prepared for a rabbit hole. The SCP Foundation is more than just a collection of scary stories; it's a collaborative experiment in world-building, a testament to the power of shared imagination, and a fascinating exploration of how humanity might react to the truly inexplicable.

## 8. Common Pitfalls and Misconceptions

*   **"SCPs are just monsters":** While many are, many others are objects, locations, concepts, or phenomena. The definition of "anomaly" is broad.
*   **"The Foundation is evil":** It's more complicated. The Foundation is utilitarian, prioritizing the greater good, often through harsh means. Its morality is a central point of debate.
*   **"Keter means most dangerous":** Keter refers to containment difficulty. A Keter SCP might be hard to keep in its box but relatively harmless if it escapes, while a Safe SCP could be an instant "game over" if mishandled.
*   **"All SCPs are scary":** Many are humorous, intriguing, or even poignant. The tone varies wildly.

## 9. Conclusion: The Enduring Legacy of the Veil

The SCP Foundation represents a unique literary and cultural phenomenon. It's a universe built by thousands, bound by a common premise: that the world is stranger and more dangerous than we know, and someone has to keep the darkness at bay. Its blend of scientific rigor, bureaucratic procedure, existential horror, and boundless creativity has allowed it to thrive and evolve for over a decade.

Whether you see it as a collection of creepypastas, a philosophical exploration of secrecy and control, or simply a source of fascinating stories, the SCP Foundation offers a deep and engaging world. It reminds us that even in an age of information, mystery can persist, and that sometimes, the most important work is done in the shadows, by those willing to make difficult choices to secure, contain, and protect. The veil may be thin, but for now, the Foundation holds it in place.
