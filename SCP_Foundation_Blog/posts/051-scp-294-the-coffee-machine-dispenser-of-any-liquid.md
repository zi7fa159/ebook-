# SCP-294: The Coffee Machine - Dispenser of Any Conceivable Liquid

## 1. Introduction: Your Wish is Its Beverage

SCP-294, affectionately or apprehensively known as "The Coffee Machine," is a Euclid-class anomalous object that outwardly resembles a standard office coffee vending machine. Its extraordinary capability, however, lies in its ability to dispense almost any liquid that can be conceived of when a request is entered via its QWERTY keyboard interface, provided the user has the correct change (fifty US cents at the time of original documentation). From mundane beverages to abstract concepts, hazardous substances, and even liquids that should not exist, SCP-294's potential is as vast as language itself.

This article will explore the workings of SCP-294, its operational quirks, the fascinating and often dangerous results from its experiment logs, and the containment considerations for a machine that can pour you a cup of "perfectly brewed coffee" or "liquid gold."

## 2. Physical Description: Unassuming Vending Unit

SCP-294's appearance is deliberately unremarkable:
*   **Standard Coffee Vending Machine:** It has the typical facade of such machines, with a slot for coin insertion, a numeric keypad (though this is non-functional for liquid selection), a dispensing alcove, and a QWERTY keyboard for inputting requests.
*   **No Brand Markings:** No manufacturer's name or branding is visible on the machine.
*   **Internal Workings (Unknown):** Attempts to examine its internal components have been met with resistance or have proven inconclusive. It does not appear to have any internal storage tanks for liquids or ingredients beyond what a standard coffee machine might hold for basic coffee, tea, or hot chocolate. Its ability to produce a near-infinite variety of liquids is therefore highly anomalous.
*   **Location:** Housed in the 2nd floor personnel lounge at Site-██ (or a similar common area in various Foundation facilities, depending on the tale or interpretation).

Its innocuous appearance allows it to blend into a typical office environment, making its anomalous nature all the more surprising.

## 3. Anomalous Properties: The Liquid Lexicon

SCP-294's primary anomalous property is its response to typed requests:
*   **QWERTY Keyboard Input:** Users type their desired liquid onto the English QWERTY keyboard.
*   **Coin Operation:** Upon insertion of fifty US cents (or equivalent in other currencies, though this is often specified as USD), the machine processes the request.
*   **Dispensing Mechanism:** If the request is accepted, SCP-294 dispenses approximately 12 fluid ounces (around 350 ml) of the requested liquid into a standard 12-ounce styrofoam cup that it also provides.
*   **Vast Range of Liquids:** SCP-294 can produce an astonishing variety of liquids:
    *   **Mundane Beverages:** Coffee (customized in many ways), tea, soda, juice, water from specific locations (e.g., "Loch Ness water").
    *   **Non-Beverages:** Molten metals (dispensed at a safe temperature), crude oil, liquid nitrogen, acids, bodily fluids (blood of a specific person, saliva, tears).
    *   **Abstract Concepts:** This is where SCP-294 becomes truly bizarre. It can attempt to dispense liquids representing abstract concepts like "a cup of music," "liquid knowledge," "my favorite memory," "a cup of joe (referring to a person named Joe)," or "the meaning of life." The results are often unpredictable, sometimes metaphorical, sometimes dangerous, or sometimes simply inert and unidentifiable.
    *   **Impossible or Fictional Liquids:** Liquids from fiction (e.g., "Butterbeer," "Slurm") or liquids that should not be able to exist in liquid form under standard conditions.
    *   **Hazardous Materials:** It will dispense poisons, radioactive liquids, and other dangerous substances if requested, posing a significant risk.
*   **"OUT OF RANGE" Response:** If SCP-294 cannot fulfill a request, its keypad will display "OUT OF RANGE." This can happen for several reasons:
    *   **Solid Objects:** It cannot dispense solid objects (e.g., "a cup of diamonds"). It must be a liquid or something that can exist in a liquid state.
    *   **Uniqueness/Specificity:** Requests for unique, one-of-a-kind liquids that no longer exist or are too specific might be out of range (e.g., "the last dodo's blood" if dodos are extinct and no samples remain).
    *   **Cosmic Limits (Speculative):** Extremely esoteric or universe-altering concepts might be beyond its capability.
    *   **Internal "Radius" Limit (Speculative):** Some theories suggest SCP-294 retrieves liquids from existing sources within a certain, possibly vast, radius. If the requested liquid doesn't exist within that radius, it's "OUT OF RANGE." This is supported by incidents where requesting "a cup of SCP-███'s bodily fluid" resulted in a substance later confirmed to be stolen from a nearby containment cell.

## 4. Object Class: Euclid

SCP-294 is classified as **Euclid**.

*   **Why Euclid?**
    *   **Potential for Hazardous Output:** Its ability to dispense dangerous substances (poisons, acids, radioactive materials, biohazards) makes it a significant safety risk if misused.
    *   **Unpredictable Abstract Outputs:** Requests for abstract concepts can yield unknown, potentially harmful, or reality-altering liquids.
    *   **Resource Theft (Potential):** If it indeed sources liquids from existing locations, it could inadvertently (or deliberately, if it has some form of intelligence) steal valuable or dangerous substances from within or outside the Foundation. The incident where it dispensed "a cup of SCP-075's corrosive fluid" from its containment cell is a prime example.
    *   **Requires Supervision:** Access and use must be carefully monitored and restricted to prevent accidents or malicious use.
*   **Why Not Safe?** Despite being a stationary object, the inherent danger of its potential outputs and the unknown mechanism of its function prevent a Safe classification.
*   **Why Not Keter?** It does not actively try to breach containment or cause widespread harm on its own. Its threat is conditional upon user input.

## 5. Special Containment Procedures: Guarding the Cosmic Vending Machine

Containment procedures for SCP-294 focus on restricting access and managing its use:
*   **Location:** Typically kept in a monitored, medium-security personnel lounge or break room.
*   **Armed Guard:** At least two armed guards with Level 2 clearance are to be stationed nearby at all times.
*   **Restricted Access:** Use of SCP-294 is restricted to personnel with Level 2 clearance or higher.
*   **Request Logging:** All requests made to SCP-294 must be logged, detailing the requested liquid, the personnel making the request, and the observed result.
*   **Prohibition of Hazardous Requests:** Personnel are generally forbidden from requesting liquids known to be dangerous (e.g., "a cup of antimatter," "liquid SCP-682 tissue") outside of approved, high-level experimental protocols. Accidental or malicious requests for such substances are a primary concern.
*   **Standard Cups Only:** Only the styrofoam cups dispensed by SCP-294 are to be used, as introducing other containers could have unknown effects or bypass safety measures (e.g., temperature regulation for molten metals).
*   **Payment Required:** The fifty-cent payment must be made for each dispensing.

The procedures aim to allow for some level of morale-boosting use (e.g., getting a perfect cup of coffee) while heavily mitigating the risks of its more dangerous capabilities.

## 6. Experiment Logs: The Heart of SCP-294's Appeal

Much like SCP-914 ("The Clockworks"), a significant part of SCP-294's fame and appeal comes from its extensive **Experiment Log 294**. This log documents the wide array of requests made to the machine and their often surprising results.
*   **Creativity and Humor:** The logs showcase the imaginative (and sometimes foolish) requests made by Foundation personnel, leading to humorous, bizarre, or enlightening outcomes.
*   **Testing Limits:** Researchers constantly test the boundaries of what SCP-294 can produce, from the mundane to the highly conceptual.
*   **Notable Requests and Results (Illustrative):**
    *   **"Cup of gold":** Dispensed molten gold at a safe-to-handle temperature.
    *   **"Cup of surprise":** Dispensed a cup of water with a small, harmless plastic toy inside.
    *   **"Cup of music":** Dispensed a clear liquid that, when consumed, caused the drinker to hear music.
    *   **"Diamond":** "OUT OF RANGE" (as it's a solid).
    *   **"Liquid Diamond":** Dispensed carbon, but not in a liquid diamond state.
    *   **"What SCP-682 would find delicious":** "OUT OF RANGE" (implying even SCP-294 has limits or safety protocols against truly catastrophic requests).
    *   **"Joe":** After clarification ("a cup of Joe Miller"), it dispensed blood, saliva, and other bodily fluids later matched to a Site Director named Joe Miller. This highlighted its potential for sourcing biological matter.
    *   **"Christ's Blood":** Dispensed red wine, with the implication that this is what it "found" associated with the concept.
    *   **"A cup of 'wow!'":** Often results in something unexpected and subjectively impressive or surprising to the requester.
*   **Cross-SCP Interactions:** Requests involving other SCPs (e.g., "water from SCP-006," "fluid from SCP-173's cell") are common, sometimes leading to important discoveries or dangerous situations.

The experiment logs are a testament to the Foundation's (and the SCP community's) creativity and the endless possibilities SCP-294 represents.

## 7. Theories on SCP-294's Mechanism

How SCP-294 actually works is a complete mystery:
*   **Materialization/Creation?** Does it synthesize liquids from raw matter or energy?
*   **Teleportation/Retrieval?** Does it locate and teleport existing liquids from somewhere in the universe (or multiverse)? The "Joe Miller" incident and others support this theory. If so, what are its range and limitations?
*   **Conceptual Manifestation?** For abstract requests, how does it translate a concept like "knowledge" or "surprise" into a liquid form? Is it tapping into some kind of conceptual reality or collective unconscious?
*   **Sentience?** Does SCP-294 possess some form of intelligence or understanding of the requests it receives, or is it merely a complex, anomalous input-output device? Its occasional witty or metaphorical responses to abstract requests hint at some level of understanding.

These unanswered questions make it a continuously intriguing object of study.

## 8. Conclusion: The Ultimate Beverage Dispenser, With Caveats

SCP-294, "The Coffee Machine," is a beloved and classic SCP that perfectly encapsulates the Foundation's blend of the mundane and the extraordinary. It takes a familiar office object and imbues it with near-limitless potential, capable of dispensing anything from a perfect latte to the literal stuff of nightmares or dreams. Its experiment logs are a wellspring of creativity, humor, and occasional horror, showcasing the endless curiosity of Foundation researchers and the unpredictable nature of anomalies.

While its Euclid classification is due to the inherent risks of its more dangerous potential outputs, SCP-294 often serves as a source of minor convenience and morale in the stressful environment of a Foundation Site. It remains a potent reminder that in the SCP universe, even the most unassuming objects can hold profound and reality-altering power. Just be careful what you wish for (and have 50 cents ready). You might just get exactly what you asked for, in liquid form.
