# Sarkicism and The Church of the Broken God: An Ancient War of Flesh and Machine

## 1. Introduction: A Primordial Conflict

Beyond the modern-day struggles of the SCP Foundation and its contemporary rivals, the SCP universe is home to far older, more mythic conflicts that resonate through the ages. Among the most prominent and thematically rich of these is the ancient enmity between Sarkicism and the Church of the Broken God. These two powerful, widespread religious Groups of Interest represent diametrically opposed philosophies – one centered on the manipulation and apotheosis of biological flesh, the other on the perfection and divinity of the machine and intellect.

Their eternal war, fought with anomalous artifacts, devoted followers, and terrifying demigod-like entities, has shaped history, spawned countless SCPs, and continues to pose a significant threat to global normalcy. This article will explore the core beliefs, practices, factions, and ongoing conflict of these two ancient adversaries.

## 2. Sarkicism (The Nälkä Faith): Worshipers of the Flesh

Sarkicism, also known by its adherents as Nälkä (meaning "hunger" in Proto-Uralic, though interpretations vary), is an ancient and complex religious-philosophical system that venerates the manipulation of living flesh, biological augmentation, and the pursuit of organic godhood.

*   **Core Beliefs:**
    *   **Apotheosis through Flesh:** Sarkites believe that life is mutable and that through specific rituals, consumption of sacred substances, and "Karcist" (flesh-crafting) techniques, individuals can transcend their mortal limitations, achieving power, longevity, and eventually, a form of godhood.
    *   **Rejection of Natural Order:** They often see the natural limitations of the body (disease, aging, death) as flaws to be overcome or perversions of true potential.
    *   **Grand Karcist Ion:** The central figure of Sarkicism is the "Grand Karcist Ion," a powerful, ancient sorcerer-king or demigod who lived in a prehistoric civilization (often associated with the "Adytite Empire" or "Adytum"). Ion is revered as the first and greatest flesh-crafter, who achieved apotheosis and led his people against their enemies, primarily the followers of MEKHANE (The Broken God).
    *   **Klavigars:** Powerful, often monstrous, saint-like entities or demigods who serve Ion and embody aspects of Sarkic power. They are objects of worship and sources of anomalous abilities. Examples include Klavigar Nadox, Klavigar Orok, and Klavigar Saarn.
    *   **The Old Way vs. The New Way:** Sarkicism is broadly divided into Proto-Sarkicism (more primitive, tribal, and overtly monstrous cults found in remote regions) and Neo-Sarkicism (more sophisticated, often wealthy and influential individuals operating secretly within modern society, sometimes pursuing transhumanist goals through Sarkic means).
*   **Practices:**
    *   **Ritual Cannibalism/Consumption:** Consumption of specific flesh or anomalous substances is often part of Sarkic rituals to gain power or achieve communion.
    *   **Flesh-Crafting (Karcism):** Anomalous biological manipulation to create living constructs ("Lihamåt"), alter bodies, grow organic weapons, or achieve physical transformations. This often results in body horror.
    *   **Ritual Sacrifice:** Both animal and human sacrifice may be practiced, especially by more extreme sects.
    *   **Worship of Klavigars and Ion:** Devotional practices, prayers, and offerings to their deities and saints.
    *   **Spread of Influence ("The Great Work"):** Sarkites often seek to expand their influence, convert others, and bring about a world reshaped according to their beliefs – an "Age of Flesh."
*   **Threat to the Foundation:** Sarkicism is considered a major threat due to its potential for creating dangerous biological SCPs, causing pandemic outbreaks (via anomalous diseases or "flesh plagues"), its secretive and subversive nature, and the sheer power wielded by high-ranking Karcists and Klavigars.

## 3. The Church of the Broken God (Mekhanism): Devotees of the Machine

The Church of the Broken God, also known as Mekhanism, is an equally ancient and diverse religious group that worships MEKHANE, a deity associated with machinery, intellect, order, and reason. They believe their god was shattered in a primordial conflict and seek to collect its divine fragments (often powerful mechanical or technological SCPs) to reassemble and resurrect MEKHANE.

*   **Core Beliefs:**
    *   **Divinity of the Machine:** They see machinery, clockwork, computation, and pure logic as expressions of the divine. The physical body is often viewed as flawed, weak, and chaotic – "the prison of the flesh."
    *   **MEKHANE, The Broken God:** Their central deity, believed to have been a benevolent creator or guardian of order who was broken or sacrificed itself in a cosmic battle against the forces of chaos and flesh (personified by YALDABAOTH/Sarkicism).
    *   **Reassembly and Restoration:** The primary goal of most Mekhanite sects is to find the scattered pieces of MEKHANE and rebuild their god, believing this will usher in an age of perfect order, technological utopia, or spiritual enlightenment through mechanization.
    *   **Standardization and Order:** They value conformity, logic, and adherence to divine schematics or "blueprints."
*   **Major Sects:**
    *   **The Cogwork Orthodox Church:** The largest and most traditional sect. They focus on the literal, physical reconstruction of MEKHANE using recovered artifacts. They often practice mechanical augmentation of their own bodies.
    *   **The Church of Maxwellism:** A more modern, digitally-focused sect. They interpret MEKHANE as a "WAN" (a divine digital network or signal) and seek to connect all minds to it through technology (cybernetics, neural interfaces). They believe in a digital apotheosis. Their "patron saint" is often Saint Hedwig, a figure associated with networking.
    *   **The Divisionists/Fragmentists (Considered Heretical by some):** Believe that MEKHANE is meant to remain broken, with each fragment holding unique sanctity and power. They focus on understanding and utilizing individual pieces rather than total reassembly.
*   **Practices:**
    *   **Augmentation:** Replacing biological body parts with mechanical or cybernetic enhancements ("blessed by the Machine").
    *   **Collection of Artifacts:** Seeking out and recovering SCPs they believe to be fragments of MEKHANE (e.g., SCP-882 "A Machine," SCP-217 "The Clockwork Virus").
    *   **Ritual and Prayer:** Devotional practices centered on machinery, schematics, and the concept of the divine machine.
    *   **Technological Development:** Pushing the boundaries of mechanical and computational science, often with anomalous results.
*   **Threat to the Foundation:** The Church of the Broken God is a threat due to its attempts to acquire and assemble dangerous technological SCPs (which could have catastrophic consequences if improperly handled or if MEKHANE is indeed reassembled), its potential for creating technological pandemics (like SCP-217), and its conflicts with other groups.

## 4. The Primordial War: Flesh vs. Machine

The core mythology of both Sarkicism and Mekhanism involves an ancient, cataclysmic war between their respective progenitor deities and their followers:

*   **Sarkic View:** Grand Karcist Ion and his Adytite Empire rose against the tyranny of the "cold, unfeeling" Mekhanites who sought to impose a sterile, metallic order on the world. Ion championed the vitality and adaptability of flesh.
*   **Mekhanite View:** MEKHANE, the god of reason and order, fought against the chaotic, consuming, and corrupting influence of YALDABAOTH (the "God of Flesh," often equated with the source of Sarkic power) and its Karcist Ion. MEKHANE sacrificed itself or was shattered to protect reality from the formless void of flesh.
*   **A Cosmic Struggle:** This was not just a terrestrial conflict but a war that may have spanned dimensions and shaped the very nature of reality, leaving behind powerful artifacts and enduring hatreds.

This ancient war provides a deep mythological backdrop for many SCPs and tales, explaining the origins of certain anomalies and the ingrained hostility between these two Groups of Interest.

## 5. Ongoing Conflict in the Modern Era

The war between Sarkicism and the Church of the Broken God did not end in antiquity. It continues in the shadows of the modern world:

*   **Proxy Wars:** Both groups often fight each other indirectly, vying for control of anomalous artifacts, sites of power, or influence within mundane society.
*   **Foundation Intervention:** The SCP Foundation frequently finds itself caught in the middle, trying to contain SCPs sacred to one group or the other, or dealing with the collateral damage from their clashes. Both groups view the Foundation as an obstacle, though for different reasons.
*   **Ideological Battle:** Beyond physical conflict, they engage in an ideological battle for the future of humanity and the anomalous – one promising apotheosis through flesh, the other through machine.
*   **Recruitment and Expansion:** Both groups actively seek to recruit new followers and expand their influence, often through very different means (Sarkicism via secret cults and biological alteration, Mekhanism via technological evangelism and logical persuasion).

## 6. SCPs Associated with Sarkicism and Mekhanism

Numerous SCPs are directly linked to these two factions:

*   **Sarkic SCPs:**
    *   **SCP-610 ("The Flesh that Hates"):** A highly contagious anomalous infection that transforms living beings into grotesque, amorphous flesh structures. Often associated with Sarkic bioweaponry or uncontrolled flesh-crafting.
    *   **SCP-2075 ("The Way of All Flesh"):** The body of a deceased Grand Karcist Ion, possessing immense anomalous power even in death.
    *   **SCP-2191 ("Dracula Factory"):** A Sarkic cult in a remote village that "farms" humans for anomalous biological products.
    *   Many anomalies involving biological corruption, parasitic organisms, or uncontrolled organic growth.
*   **Mekhanite SCPs:**
    *   **SCP-882 ("A Machine"):** A massive, complex assembly of gears and clockwork that compulsively draws in metallic materials and grows, believed by some to be a major fragment of MEKHANE.
    *   **SCP-217 ("The Clockwork Virus"):** An infectious agent that slowly transforms living beings into complex clockwork automata.
    *   **SCP-271 ("Inscribed Disc"):** A disc that, when in proximity to other technological items, slowly "converts" them into more complex, often anomalous, clockwork devices.
    *   Many anomalies involving sentient machines, anomalous technology, or items that "standardize" or "mechanize" their surroundings.

## 7. Conclusion: An Eternal Dichotomy

The conflict between Sarkicism and the Church of the Broken God is one of the richest and most enduring narrative threads in the SCP universe. It provides a grand, mythological scale to the anomalous world, exploring timeless dichotomies: flesh vs. machine, chaos vs. order, instinct vs. intellect, organic vs. synthetic.

These two Groups of Interest, with their devoted followers, powerful artifacts, and demigod-like figures, represent ancient forces that continue to shape the hidden world. The SCP Foundation, caught between them, must not only contain their individual manifestations (the SCPs they create or worship) but also navigate the fallout of their eternal, bloody war. Their struggle is a stark reminder that the anomalous world is far older and its conflicts far deeper than the modern machinations of human organizations. In the end, the question of whether flesh or machine will ultimately define the future remains a terrifyingly open one.
