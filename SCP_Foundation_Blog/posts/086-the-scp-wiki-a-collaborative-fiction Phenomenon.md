# The SCP Wiki: A Universe Forged by Collaborative Fiction

## 1. Introduction: Beyond a Single Author

The SCP Foundation, with its sprawling universe of anomalous objects, clandestine organizations, and intricate lore, is not the creation of a single author or a small team. It is a massive, ongoing collaborative fiction project, primarily hosted on the SCP Wiki website (scp-wiki.wikidot.com). Thousands of writers, artists, and community members from around the globe contribute to this shared universe, creating a unique and ever-expanding tapestry of horror, science fiction, and speculative fiction.

This article explores the phenomenon of the SCP Wiki itself: its origins, its collaborative nature, its community structure, its creative principles, and how it has grown from a single creepypasta post into one of the internet's most significant and influential examples of emergent, shared-world storytelling.

## 2. Origins: From SCP-173 to a Universe

The SCP Foundation project began modestly:
*   **The Spark (2007):** The very first SCP article, SCP-173 ("The Sculpture"), was posted anonymously on the /x/ (paranormal) board of 4chan. It featured a creepy image (Izumi Kato's "Untitled 2004") and a concise, clinical description of a statue that moves when unobserved and snaps necks.
*   **Early Growth:** The unique format and compelling horror of SCP-173 inspired others on /x/ to create their own "SCP" entries, mimicking the clinical tone and "Special Containment Procedures" format.
*   **The First Wiki (EditThis):** As more SCPs were created, they were initially collected on an EditThis wiki page in early 2008 to organize them. This allowed for easier collaboration and expansion.
*   **Move to Wikidot (July 2008):** Due to limitations and issues with EditThis, the community migrated the project to its current home on Wikidot. This platform offered better tools for organization, moderation, and community building, allowing the SCP universe to truly flourish.

From a single post, a universe was born, driven by collective creativity.

## 3. The Collaborative Model: Shared World, Diverse Voices

The core of the SCP Wiki's success is its collaborative model:
*   **Open Contribution (with rules):** Anyone can apply to become a member of the SCP Wiki and, once accepted, can contribute by writing new SCP articles, tales (narrative stories set in the SCP universe), Group of Interest formats, artwork, or by participating in critiques and discussions.
*   **No Single Canon:** There is no single, overarching "official" canon. While some articles and characters become highly popular and widely referenced (forming "headcanons" or widely accepted lore), authors are generally free to interpret, contradict, or build upon existing material in their own works. This allows for immense diversity and prevents the universe from becoming creatively stagnant. Multiple SCP-001 proposals are a prime example of this.
*   **Peer Review and Quality Control:**
    *   **Critique Process:** Authors are strongly encouraged (and often required for new writers) to submit their drafts for critique by other community members in forums or chatrooms before posting to the main list.
    *   **Voting System:** Articles on the main list are subject to a rating system (upvotes/downvotes). If an article falls below a certain negative threshold (typically -10), it is subject to deletion by site staff. This helps maintain a general standard of quality.
    *   **Site Staff/Moderators:** A dedicated team of volunteer staff (administrators, moderators, operational staff) manage the site, enforce rules, handle disputes, and oversee technical aspects.
*   **Shared Universe, Individual Ownership:** While authors contribute to a shared universe, they generally retain creative ownership of their specific characters and articles (within the bounds of the site's Creative Commons license).

This model balances creative freedom with community-driven quality control.

## 4. Key Components of the SCP Wiki

The SCP Wiki is more than just a list of SCP articles:
*   **SCP Articles (The Mainlist):** The core of the project. Each article describes an anomalous object, entity, location, or phenomenon using the standardized "Special Containment Procedures" format (Item #, Object Class, Special Containment Procedures, Description, Addenda). There are currently thousands of these, from SCP-001 up to SCP-7999 (and beyond, with new series opening as old ones fill).
*   **Tales:** Narrative fiction set within the SCP universe. These can explore the lives of Foundation personnel, the history of anomalies, interactions between Groups of Interest, specific events, or deeper philosophical themes. Tales allow for more traditional storytelling styles.
*   **GoI Formats:** Articles written from the perspective of other Groups of Interest (e.g., a GOC parathreat assessment, a Serpent's Hand library entry, an MC&D auction catalog). These expand the universe and offer different viewpoints.
*   **Canons and Series:** Collections of articles and tales that are explicitly interconnected or explore a shared storyline or theme (e.g., "The Ouroboros Cycle," "End of Death," "Broken Masquerade," "Antimemetics Division Hub"). Canons are often opt-in by authors.
*   **Artwork and Multimedia:** A thriving community of artists creates visual depictions of SCPs, characters, and scenes, often hosted on the site or linked from articles. Audio dramas, animations, and short films also exist.
*   **Essays and Guides:** Informative pages about writing for the SCP Wiki, understanding specific lore elements, site rules, and the history of the community.
*   **Forums and Discussion Areas:** Where community members interact, critique drafts, propose ideas, and discuss all aspects of the SCP universe.
*   **International Branches:** The SCP Wiki has numerous official international translation branches (e.g., Russian, Korean, Chinese, Japanese, French, Spanish, Polish, Thai, German, Italian, Ukrainian, Portuguese, Czech, Traditional Chinese, Vietnamese), each with its own community, original content, and translations of English-branch articles. This makes SCP a truly global phenomenon.

## 5. Creative Commons Licensing: The Engine of Expansion

A crucial aspect of the SCP Wiki's success and its ability to inspire derivative works (like video games, YouTube channels, etc.) is its licensing:
*   **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 License (CC BY-SA 3.0):** All content on the SCP Wiki is licensed under this Creative Commons license. This means:
    *   **Attribution:** Anyone can use or adapt SCP content, even commercially, as long as they give proper credit to the original authors and the SCP Wiki.
    *   **ShareAlike:** Any derivative works created must also be licensed under the same or a compatible ShareAlike license.
*   **Impact:** This open licensing model has allowed the SCP universe to spread far beyond the confines of the wiki itself, fostering a massive ecosystem of fan creations, games (like "SCP - Containment Breach," "SCP: Secret Laboratory"), animations, and online content, which in turn draws new readers and writers to the original source.

The CC BY-SA license is fundamental to the SCP project's collaborative and expansive nature.

## 6. The SCP Community: Culture and Challenges

The community around the SCP Wiki is diverse, passionate, and has its own unique culture:
*   **Emphasis on Quality Writing:** There's a strong focus on good writing, originality, and effective horror (or other genres). The critique process, while sometimes harsh, aims to improve submissions.
*   **Lore Keepers and Newcomers:** A mix of long-time members with deep knowledge of established lore and new writers bringing fresh perspectives.
*   **Internal "Politics" and Debates:** Like any large online community, there can be disagreements about site policy, the direction of the lore, or the quality of certain content. Site staff work to manage these.
*   **Inclusivity Efforts:** The community has made significant efforts over the years to become more inclusive and welcoming to diverse voices and identities.
*   **Challenges:**
    *   **Maintaining Quality with Growth:** As the site grows, ensuring a consistent level of quality across thousands of articles is a constant challenge.
    *   **Canon Cohesion (or lack thereof):** The "no single canon" approach is a strength but can also be confusing for newcomers or those seeking a perfectly consistent universe.
    *   **Avoiding Tropes and Clichés:** With so much content, avoiding repetition or overused ideas requires constant creativity.

## 7. The Enduring Appeal of the SCP Foundation

Why has the SCP Foundation project remained so popular and creatively fertile for over a decade?
*   **The Power of the Format:** The clinical, pseudo-scientific format is inherently compelling. It lends an air of realism and believability to even the most outlandish concepts, making the horror more effective.
*   **Endless Creative Possibilities:** The premise of "anomalies" is incredibly broad, allowing for stories in virtually any genre – horror, sci-fi, fantasy, comedy, tragedy, mystery, philosophical exploration.
*   **Shared World-Building:** The joy of contributing to and exploring a vast, interconnected universe built by many hands is a powerful draw.
*   **The Allure of Secrets and Conspiracies:** The Foundation taps into our fascination with secret organizations, hidden truths, and things humanity was "not meant to know."
*   **Active and Evolving Community:** The constant influx of new ideas, new authors, and new interpretations keeps the universe fresh and dynamic.
*   **Adaptability:** The SCP concept has proven highly adaptable to other media (games, videos, art), allowing it to reach wider audiences.

## 8. Conclusion: A Living, Breathing Universe of Shared Nightmares (and Wonders)

The SCP Wiki is more than just a collection of scary stories; it is a living, breathing testament to the power of collaborative fiction and online community. From its humble beginnings as a single creepypasta, it has evolved into a sprawling multiverse of interconnected narratives, maintained and expanded by the passion and creativity of thousands of contributors worldwide. Its unique blend of clinical detachment and imaginative horror, its open licensing, and its "no single canon" philosophy have allowed it to become a cultural phenomenon and a landmark in internet-based storytelling.

The SCP Foundation, as documented on its wiki, is a universe constantly being written, rewritten, and reinterpreted. It is a place where anyone with a compelling idea and the skill to articulate it can add their own anomalous thread to an ever-growing tapestry of fear, wonder, and mystery. The doors of the Foundation are always open (to new writers, if not to the public), and the database is always hungry for new entries, ensuring that the lights at the SCP Wiki will likely stay on, cataloging the darkness, for a long time to come.
