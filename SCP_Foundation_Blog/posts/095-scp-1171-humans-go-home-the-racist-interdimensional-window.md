# SCP-1171: Humans Go Home! - The Racist Interdimensional Window Entity

## 1. Introduction: An Unlikely Portal for Bigotry

SCP-1171, commonly known by the phrase "Humans Go Home" (often associated with its primary manifestation), is a Euclid-class anomalous phenomenon centered around a house whose windows act as intermittent portals to an extradimensional, non-human entity. This entity, referring to itself as "Beauregard" (among other names), is sapient, capable of communication by writing on its side of the windowpane (which appears as condensation on the Foundation's side), and harbors a deep-seated, irrational prejudice against human beings, whom it derogatorily refers to as "meat."

This article explores the peculiar nature of SCP-1171, the characteristics of the entity Beauregard, the bizarre "conversations" held with it, and the SCP's subtle but effective use of an alien perspective to satirize and critique real-world prejudice and xenophobia.

## 2. The Anomaly: A House with a View (to Another Dimension)

The primary physical component of SCP-1171 is a specific residential house:
*   **Location:** A two-story house located in █████, Queensland, Australia.
*   **Anomalous Windows:** Certain windows in the house, particularly on the upper floor, will periodically become clouded with condensation on the *exterior* surface, regardless of ambient temperature or humidity. This condensation is where SCP-1171-1 (Beauregard) writes its messages.
*   **The "Other Side":** Looking through these clouded windows when the anomaly is active does not show the normal exterior view. Instead, it reveals a dimly lit, alien landscape or room – the environment where SCP-1171-1 resides. This environment is often described as being orange-tinged, with strange architecture or unidentifiable features. Direct visual observation of SCP-1171-1 is difficult or impossible; only its writing is clearly seen.

The house itself is otherwise non-anomalous, but it serves as the fixed point of contact with the extradimensional entity.

## 3. SCP-1171-1 ("Beauregard"): The Xenophobic Entity

The entity that communicates through the windows is designated SCP-1171-1:
*   **Self-Identification:** Refers to itself by various names, including "Beauregard," "Pester Japsers," "The Grand Pester," and others. "Beauregard" is the most commonly used by Foundation personnel.
*   **Nature (Speculative):** Its true form is unknown, as it is never clearly seen. It is sapient, intelligent (though deeply prejudiced), and possesses a distinct, often cantankerous, personality. It is likely non-humanoid and from a species with a different biology and culture.
*   **Communication Method:** Writes messages on its side of the windowpane. These messages appear as if written in condensation on the Foundation's side of the glass, allowing for two-way (albeit slow) communication if Foundation personnel write back on their side.
*   **Language:** Communicates in English, though with occasional misspellings, colloquialisms, or idiosyncratic phrasing.
*   **Core Characteristic: Anti-Human Prejudice:** SCP-1171-1 harbors an extreme and irrational prejudice against human beings.
    *   **Derogatory Terminology:** Refers to humans as "meat," "flesh-sacks," "primates," "apes," "vermin," or other insulting terms.
    *   **Stereotypes and Conspiracy Theories:** Expresses bizarre and unfounded stereotypes about humans (e.g., "all humans are secretly cannibals," "humans control the media to spread pro-meat propaganda," "humans are trying to invade our dimension").
    *   **Fear of "Contamination":** Seems to fear being "contaminated" by human presence or ideas.
    *   **"Humans Go Home!":** A frequent message, indicating a desire for humans to stay out of its dimension or sphere of influence.
*   **Other Personality Traits:**
    *   **Grumpy and Opinionated:** Often complains about various topics (its equivalent of "the weather," "the government," "youngsters these days").
    *   **Curious (Selectively):** Despite its prejudice, it sometimes shows curiosity about human culture or specific human individuals it interacts with, though this is usually filtered through its biased lens.
    *   **Possesses a "Family":** Mentions having a "brood-mother," "spawnlings," and other familial connections, suggesting a social structure.
    *   **Concerned about Property Values:** One of its recurring complaints is that the presence of the "human portal" (SCP-1171) is lowering property values in its neighborhood.

Beauregard is essentially an alien bigot, a cosmic NIMBY.

## 4. Interactions and "Conversations"

The SCP-1171 article includes logs of interactions between Foundation personnel (often Dr. Character, a recurring researcher in the logs) and Beauregard. These interactions highlight:
*   **The Absurdity of Prejudice:** Beauregard's racist arguments against humans are often nonsensical, based on bizarre misunderstandings, or are direct parallels to real-world racist tropes, simply with "humans" substituted for the targeted group. This creates a satirical effect.
*   **Attempts at Dialogue:** Foundation personnel attempt to engage Beauregard in rational conversation, correct its misconceptions, or understand its culture, usually with limited success due to its ingrained prejudice.
*   **Beauregard's "Logic":** Its reasoning is often flawed, circular, and based on confirmation bias. It interprets any human action or statement through the lens of its anti-human bigotry.
*   **Moments of (Misguided) Connection:** Occasionally, Beauregard might express something that seems like a relatable concern (e.g., worrying about its spawnlings' future) before twisting it back into an anti-human rant.
*   **Humor and Pathos:** The interactions can be darkly humorous due to the sheer absurdity of Beauregard's statements, but also carry an undercurrent of pathos, as it represents a mind trapped by irrational hatred.

The logs are central to SCP-1171's effectiveness as social commentary.

## 5. Object Class: Euclid

SCP-1171 is classified as **Euclid**.
*   **Why Euclid?**
    *   **Unpredictable Manifestations:** The window phenomenon and Beauregard's appearances are intermittent and cannot be reliably predicted.
    *   **Extradimensional Entity:** Dealing with an entity from another dimension whose full capabilities and nature are unknown carries inherent risks.
    *   **Potential for Information Breach (Minor):** While Beauregard is prejudiced, it *is* an alien intelligence. There's a remote possibility it could reveal dangerous information about its dimension or technology, or that its dimension could pose a threat if the "portal" became more stable or two-way for physical matter.
    *   **Psychological Impact (Minor):** Prolonged interaction with a hostile, prejudiced entity could be stressful for personnel, though this is not its primary danger.
*   **Why Not Safe?** The unpredictability and the unknown nature of the extradimensional connection prevent a Safe classification.
*   **Why Not Keter?** Beauregard poses no direct physical threat. It cannot cross into our dimension, nor can its writings cause direct harm. Its "threat" is primarily one of offensive ideology and a very minor risk of information leakage.

## 6. Special Containment Procedures: Maintaining the Unfriendly Neighborhood Watch

Containment of SCP-1171 focuses on securing the house and managing interactions with Beauregard:
*   **Acquisition of Property:** The Foundation has purchased the house where SCP-1171 is located and established a permanent observation post (Observation Post 1171-Alpha) within it.
*   **Monitoring Windows:** The anomalous windows are under constant surveillance for manifestations of SCP-1171-1.
*   **Communication Protocol:**
    *   Designated personnel (often with psychological resilience and training in handling prejudiced individuals, however alien) are assigned to communicate with SCP-1171-1 when it appears.
    *   All communications are logged.
    *   Personnel are instructed to remain polite and non-confrontational (as much as possible) to encourage continued communication and gather intelligence, despite Beauregard's offensive rhetoric.
*   **No Provocation:** Personnel are not to deliberately provoke or antagonize SCP-1171-1.
*   **Information Control:** Information about SCP-1171 is classified to prevent public knowledge of interdimensional contact, however bizarre.
*   **Research Goals:** Ongoing research aims to understand the nature of SCP-1171-1's dimension, its species, its culture (beyond its bigotry), and the mechanism of the window-portal.

The Foundation is essentially maintaining a very strange, interdimensional "neighborhood watch" on a cosmic racist.

## 7. Social Commentary and Thematic Significance

SCP-1171 is a clever piece of social commentary disguised as an anomalous phenomenon:
*   **Satire of Racism and Xenophobia:** By having an alien entity spout prejudiced rhetoric against humans using arguments and stereotypes that directly mirror real-world racism, the article highlights the absurdity, irrationality, and ignorance inherent in all forms of bigotry.
*   **The "Othering" of Humans:** It flips the script – humans, usually the ones "othering" aliens or monsters, become the target of alien prejudice. This forces a degree of uncomfortable self-reflection.
*   **The Banality of Hate:** Beauregard's prejudice is not grand or cosmic; it's petty, ignorant, and often wrapped in mundane complaints (like property values), making it feel uncomfortably familiar.
*   **The Difficulty of Dialogue with Bigotry:** The Foundation's attempts at rational discussion are usually futile against Beauregard's entrenched beliefs, mirroring the real-world difficulty of changing a bigot's mind.
*   **Humor as a Tool for Critique:** The article uses humor effectively to expose the ridiculousness of prejudice.

## 8. Conclusion: The Alien Next Door (Who Hates You)

SCP-1171, "Humans Go Home!," is a unique and memorable SCP that uses the premise of an interdimensional window and a prejudiced alien entity to deliver sharp social satire. Beauregard, with his grumpy demeanor and bizarre anti-human rants written in condensation, is a surprisingly effective caricature of real-world bigotry. The anomaly is not physically threatening, but its psychological and social implications are thought-provoking.

Through the strange "conversations" across a misty pane of glass, SCP-1171 reminds us that prejudice is not a uniquely human failing and that the fear and hatred of "the other" can be just as potent, and just as absurd, whether it's across the street or across dimensions. It's a testament to the SCP Foundation's ability to find commentary and even humor in the most unexpected corners of the anomalous world, even if that corner is just an alien complaining that the "meat-people" are ruining his interdimensional neighborhood.
