# Chapter 2: Understanding Canary Varieties for Breeding

Before embarking on your breeding journey, it's crucial to have a foundational understanding of the diverse world of canary varieties. Canaries are not a monolithic species; centuries of selective breeding have resulted in a stunning array of types, primarily categorized by their song, color, or type (conformation/posture). Your choice of variety will significantly influence your breeding goals, the specific care requirements, and the characteristics you aim to cultivate in your offspring.

This chapter provides an overview of the main canary categories and some popular examples within each, helping you make an informed decision if you haven't already settled on a specific type, or deepening your appreciation for the variety you've chosen.

## 2.1. The Three Main Categories of Canaries

Domestic canaries ( *Serinus canaria domestica*) are generally grouped into three broad categories based on the primary traits for which they are prized:

1.  **Song Canaries:** Bred primarily for the quality, complexity, and beauty of their song.
2.  **Color Canaries:** Bred for specific colors, patterns, and the intensity of their plumage.
3.  **Type (or Form/Posture) Canaries:** Bred for their specific physical shape, size, posture, or feather characteristics.

It's important to note that there can be overlap. For example, a color canary will still sing, and a type canary will have a specific color. However, the primary focus of breeding and judging for each category differs.

## 2.2. Song Canaries: The Virtuosos

For many, the canary's song is its most enchanting feature. Song canary breeders focus meticulously on developing and refining vocal talents. Competitions for song canaries are judged on the specific notes, tours (phrases), and overall rendition of their song, often performed in specialized judging cages.

*   **Waterslager (Malinois Canary):**
    *   **Origin:** Belgium.
    *   **Song:** Known for its water-like notes ("water slagen" means "water beats"). The song is characterized by bubbling, rolling, and flute-like sounds, often described as soft and liquid. They have specific tours that are highly valued.
    *   **Appearance:** Typically yellow or white, though color is secondary to song. Medium-sized.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Requires careful selection for song quality. Young males are often tutored by accomplished singers.

*   **Spanish Timbrado:**
    *   **Origin:** Spain.
    *   **Song:** A bright, cheerful, and metallic song, considered closer to the wild canary's song. It includes a variety of tours, some continuous and some discontinuous, with a lively tempo.
    *   **Appearance:** Can come in various colors, often green, yellow, or variegated. Hardy and robust.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Valued for its natural, less "tutored" song. Good beginner song canary.

*   **German Roller (Harz Roller):**
    *   **Origin:** Harz Mountains, Germany.
    *   **Song:** A very soft, deep, and rolling song, delivered with the beak almost closed. It's known for its hollow rolls, bass notes, and flute-like tones. The song is not loud but is considered very pleasant and soothing.
    *   **Appearance:** Typically yellow, sometimes ticked or lightly variegated.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Song quality is paramount. Sensitive to noise during tutoring.

*   **American Singer:**
    *   **Origin:** United States (developed in the 1930s).
    *   **Song & Appearance:** This breed is unique as it's judged on both song (60%) and appearance (40%). The song should be pleasant, varied, and not too loud. They were developed from Roller (for song) and Border (for type/appearance) canaries.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Breeders must balance both song quality and physical conformation to meet the breed standard.

## 2.3. Color Canaries: A Living Rainbow

Color canaries are bred for the visual appeal of their plumage. This is perhaps the most diverse category, with a complex system of genetics determining feather color and patterns. They are broadly divided into **Melanin** (dark pigmented) and **Lipochrome** (light ground color) canaries.

### Lipochrome Canaries:
These birds lack dark melanin pigment in their feathers, showcasing clear, bright ground colors.

*   **Yellow Lipochrome:** The classic canary color. Intensity can vary.
*   **Red Lipochrome (Red Factor):** Achieved by introducing genes from the South American Black-hooded Red Siskin. These canaries require color feeding (e.g., with canthaxanthin or beta-carotene) during molting to achieve their vibrant red hue.
    *   **Intensive (Non-Frosted):** Feathers are colored to the very tip, giving a deep, solid appearance.
    *   **Non-Intensive (Frosted):** Feathers have a white edge, giving a softer, "frosted" look. Pairing intensive with non-intensive is a common breeding strategy.
*   **White Lipochrome:**
    *   **Dominant White:** Appears white but may have a yellow or buff tinge on the wing flights. Lethal when homozygous (two dominant white genes), so typically bred Dominant White x Yellow/Red.
    *   **Recessive White:** Pure white, lacking any lipochrome color. Requires Vitamin A supplementation as they cannot synthesize it.
*   **Other Lipochromes:** Ivory (diluted red/yellow), Rose (diluted red), Mosaic (specific pattern of color, see below).

### Melanin Canaries:
These birds display dark pigments (eumelanin - black/brown, and phaeomelanin - brown) in patterns over their lipochrome ground color.

*   **Classic Melanin:**
    *   **Green (Black Melanin on Yellow Ground):** The "wild type" color.
    *   **Blue (Black Melanin on White Ground):**
    *   **Bronze/Brown (Brown Melanin on Yellow/Red Ground):**
    *   **Cinnamon (Sex-linked Brown Melanin on Yellow/Red Ground):**
*   **Dilute Melanin (Pastel, Opal, Ino, Satiné):** These are mutations that affect the expression or distribution of melanin, creating softer or different visual effects.
    *   **Pastel:** Dilutes black melanin to gray, brown to a lighter shade.
    *   **Opal:** Gives a bluish-gray, pearly effect to the melanin markings.
    *   **Ino:** Greatly reduces eumelanin, often resulting in red-eyed birds (e.g., Phaeo-Ino, often called Phaeo).
    *   **Satiné:** A sex-linked mutation that eliminates black eumelanin and modifies brown eumelanin, resulting in fine, dark red-brown striations and red eyes.

### Mosaic Canaries:
A specific color pattern where the lipochrome color (red or yellow) is restricted to specific areas (mask, shoulders, rump, chest bar for males; eye stripes, slight shoulder/rump marks for females) on an otherwise white or pale background. This creates a striking contrast.

## 2.4. Type Canaries: Form and Feather

Type canaries are bred for their physical characteristics, such as size, shape (conformation), posture, or unique feathering. They often have very specific breed standards.

*   **Border Canary (Border Fancy):**
    *   **Origin:** Border region of England and Scotland.
    *   **Appearance:** Small (around 5.5 inches), compact, and well-rounded with a cheerful disposition. Often described as a "wee gem." Comes in various colors.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Focus on achieving the ideal shape and carriage.

*   **Gloster Canary:**
    *   **Origin:** Gloucestershire, England.
    *   **Appearance:** Small, cobby bird known for its head feathering. Two types:
        *   **Corona:** Has a circular crest of feathers on its head, like a little cap.
        *   **Consort:** Has a plain head but carries the crest gene.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Crested (Corona) birds should be bred to plain-headed (Consort) birds. Breeding Corona to Corona can result in lethal genes or feather issues.

*   **Fife Canary (Fife Fancy):**
    *   **Origin:** Fife, Scotland.
    *   **Appearance:** Similar to the Border but even smaller (around 4.5 inches), a miniature version. Known for its active nature.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Emphasis on small size and good conformation.

*   **Yorkshire Canary:**
    *   **Origin:** Yorkshire, England.
    *   **Appearance:** A large canary (up to 6.75 inches), very upright and "guardsman-like" posture. Often called the "Gentleman of the Fancy."
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Achieving the correct size, posture, and feather quality is key.

*   **Norwich Canary:**
    *   **Origin:** Norwich, England.
    *   **Appearance:** A cobby, thickset bird with a somewhat bulky appearance (around 6.5 inches). Known for its rich, deep color (often yellow or red).
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Focus on bulk, feather texture, and color.

*   **Frilled Canaries (e.g., Parisian Frill, Northern Dutch Frill, Southern Dutch Frill):**
    *   **Appearance:** Characterized by unique, curled, or frilled feathering on the chest, back, and flanks. Sizes and specific frill patterns vary by breed.
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** Feather quality and the correct placement of frills are paramount. Can sometimes be more challenging to breed due to feathering.

*   **Lizard Canary:**
    *   **Origin:** One of the oldest canary breeds.
    *   **Appearance:** Unique spangled pattern on the back feathers, resembling lizard scales, and a clear cap on the head. Comes in gold (yellow ground) or silver (white ground).
    *   **Breeding Considerations:** The clarity of the cap and the distinctness of the spangling are key. The cap size reduces with age.

## 2.5. Choosing a Variety for Breeding

When selecting a canary variety for breeding, consider:

*   **Your Primary Interest:** Are you most captivated by song, color, or form?
*   **Space and Resources:** Some larger Type canaries might require more spacious cages. Color-fed varieties require specific dietary management.
*   **Availability:** Some varieties are rarer and may be harder to acquire or find unrelated stock for.
*   **Complexity:** Color genetics can be complex but rewarding. Song training requires patience and a quiet environment. Some Type canaries have specific breeding challenges (e.g., Gloster crests).
*   **Personal Preference:** Ultimately, choose a variety that you find beautiful and engaging. Your passion for the birds will be a driving force in your breeding success.

## Chapter Summary

The world of canary varieties is rich and diverse, broadly categorized into Song, Color, and Type canaries. Song canaries like the Waterslager, Spanish Timbrado, and German Roller are bred for their unique vocal performances. Color canaries, such as Red Factors, Mosaics, and various melanin types, offer a visual feast of hues and patterns. Type canaries, including the Border, Gloster, Yorkshire, and Frills, are celebrated for their distinct physical forms and feather characteristics. Understanding these distinctions is the first step in defining your breeding goals and appreciating the specific qualities of the canaries you choose to work with.
