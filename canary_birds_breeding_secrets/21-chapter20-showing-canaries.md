# Chapter 20: Showing Your Canaries (Optional, but good value)

For many canary breeders, the journey doesn't end with successfully raising healthy chicks. The world of canary shows offers an exciting avenue to showcase the results of your hard work, receive expert feedback on your birds, connect with fellow enthusiasts, and deepen your understanding of your chosen varieties. While not a requirement for being a dedicated breeder, participating in or even just attending canary shows can add significant value and enjoyment to the hobby.

## 20.1. Why Show Your Canaries?

*   **Objective Evaluation:** Shows provide an opportunity for experienced, certified judges to evaluate your birds against the established breed standard for their variety (Type, Color, or Song). This impartial feedback is invaluable for assessing the quality of your stock and identifying areas for improvement in your breeding program.
*   **Learning Opportunity:** You'll see high-quality examples of your chosen variety and others, helping you develop a keen "eye" for what constitutes an excellent specimen. You can learn from observing how judges make their decisions and by talking to experienced exhibitors.
*   **Networking and Community:** Shows are a fantastic place to meet other breeders, share knowledge, discuss challenges, and build friendships. You can find mentors, source new stock, and become part of a supportive community.
*   **Benchmarking Your Program:** Comparing your birds to others helps you understand where your breeding program stands in terms of quality and adherence to the standard.
*   **Recognition and Achievement:** Winning awards or achieving recognition for your birds can be a source of great pride and validation for your efforts.
*   **Promoting Your Chosen Variety:** Exhibiting well-bred canaries helps promote interest and appreciation for specific breeds.

## 20.2. Understanding Canary Show Categories

Canary shows are typically divided into sections based on the main categories of canaries:

*   **Type Canaries (Form and Posture):** Judged on their body shape, size, stance, feather quality, and overall conformity to the specific breed standard (e.g., Border, Gloster, Yorkshire, Fife, Lizard, Frills). Color and song are usually secondary.
*   **Color Canaries (Colorbred):** Judged primarily on the purity, intensity, evenness, and correct expression of their color and markings according to specific color standards. Feather quality and condition are also very important. Type and song are secondary.
*   **Song Canaries (Songsters):** Judged solely on the quality, variety, and rendition of their song. Birds are often brought before judges in special covered cages and perform their song tours. Appearance is not a factor in judging. (e.g., Waterslager, German Roller, Spanish Timbrado, American Singer - though American Singers also have a conformation component).

Within these main sections, there are further classifications by specific breed, color, age (e.g., current year "unflighted" birds vs. older "flighted" birds), and sometimes sex.

## 20.3. Preparing Your Canaries for Show

Show preparation, often called "show training" or "conditioning for show," begins weeks or even months before the event.

*   **Selection of Show Candidates:**
    *   Choose birds that best represent the breed standard for their variety.
    *   They must be in perfect health and feather condition. A bird in molt or with damaged feathers will not place well.
    *   Good temperament is also important; a bird that is steady and confident in the show cage will present itself better.
*   **Show Cage Training:**
    *   Acclimate your birds to the standard show cages used for their variety. These cages are often small and uniform to allow judges to compare birds easily.
    *   Place the bird in the show cage for short periods initially, gradually increasing the time.
    *   Position the show cage where the bird can get used to activity and being observed.
    *   The goal is for the bird to be calm, perch correctly, and display its best features without being overly flighty or cowering.
*   **Diet and Condition:**
    *   Provide an excellent diet to ensure peak feather quality, color (especially for colorbreds – continue color feeding if applicable), and overall vitality.
    *   Ensure they are not too thin or too fat.
*   **Bathing:** Regular bathing helps maintain feather condition and cleanliness. Offer a bath a day or two before the show.
*   **Final Touches:**
    *   Ensure the bird is clean. Some exhibitors will gently wipe a bird's feet or beak with a damp cloth if needed.
    *   Avoid using any artificial substances on feathers to enhance color or shine – this is usually against show rules.
*   **Travel:** Transport birds securely and comfortably to the show, minimizing stress.

## 20.4. What to Expect at a Canary Show

*   **Benching:** Upon arrival, you'll "bench" your birds, meaning you place them in their assigned show cages in the designated area for their class. Show tags with entry numbers are usually attached.
*   **Judging:** Judging is typically done without the exhibitors present in the immediate judging area to ensure impartiality. Judges meticulously examine each bird, comparing it to the breed standard and to the other birds in its class.
*   **Awards:** Rosettes, ribbons, trophies, or certificates may be awarded for top placements (e.g., 1st, 2nd, 3rd in class, Best in Section, Best in Show).
*   **Sales Section:** Many shows have a sales area where breeders offer birds for sale. This can be a good place to acquire new stock.
*   **Socializing:** Take the opportunity to talk to other exhibitors, learn from their experiences, and view the winning birds.

## 20.5. Understanding Judging Criteria (General Principles)

While specific criteria vary by breed, some general principles apply:

*   **Type Canaries:** Conformation to the ideal shape and posture for the breed is paramount. Feather quality, condition, size, and "typey-ness" (how well it embodies the breed's essence) are key.
*   **Color Canaries:** Purity and depth of color, clarity of markings (for melanin birds), evenness of color distribution, and feather texture are critical. Condition and deportment also play a role.
*   **Song Canaries:** The specific song tours, their quality, harmony, absence of faults (negative notes), and overall composition of the song are judged.

**Breeder's Secret:** Obtain a copy of the official show standards for the varieties you breed. Study them carefully. This will help you understand what judges are looking for and guide your breeding selections.

## 20.6. Etiquette and Sportsmanship

*   Be courteous to show officials, judges, and fellow exhibitors.
*   Handle your birds quietly and calmly.
*   Be a good sport, whether you win or lose. Congratulate the winners.
*   Use the show as a learning experience. If your bird doesn't place well, try to understand why by comparing it to the winners and the standard, or politely ask for feedback if appropriate and allowed.

## 20.7. Starting Out in Showing

*   **Attend Shows First:** Before exhibiting, attend a few shows as a spectator. Observe the process, look at the birds, and talk to people.
*   **Join a Club:** Local and national canary clubs often host shows and provide resources for new exhibitors.
*   **Start Small:** Enter a few birds in a local or smaller show to gain experience.
*   **Seek Mentorship:** Don't be afraid to ask experienced exhibitors for advice on show preparation and selection.

## Chapter Summary

Showing canaries can be a highly rewarding extension of the breeding hobby, offering objective feedback, learning opportunities, and a strong sense of community. Success in showing requires understanding breed standards, meticulous preparation of show candidates (including cage training and conditioning), and good sportsmanship. Whether you aim for top awards or simply want to learn and connect, canary shows provide a valuable platform for any dedicated enthusiast to appreciate the diversity and beauty of these remarkable birds.
