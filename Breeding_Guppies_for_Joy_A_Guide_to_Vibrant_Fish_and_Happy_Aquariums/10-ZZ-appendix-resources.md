# Appendix: Recommended Resources for the Guppy Enthusiast

This guide has provided a comprehensive overview of breeding guppies for joy. However, the world of aquatics is ever-evolving, and continuous learning is part of the fun. Here are some recommended resources to further your knowledge and connect with the wider community.

## A.1 Online Forums and Communities

Online forums are invaluable for asking questions, sharing experiences, and learning from fellow hobbyists.

*   **Aquarium Co-Op Forum (forum.aquariumcoop.com)**: A very active and friendly community covering all aspects of fishkeeping, including livebearers. Great for beginners and experienced keepers alike.
*   **TFH Magazine Forum (tfhmagazine.com/forums)**: Tropical Fish Hobbyist Magazine's official forum, with dedicated sections for livebearers.
*   **AquariaCentral (aquariacentral.com/forums)**: Another long-standing forum with a wealth of information.
*   **Simply Guppies (simplyguppies.com)**: A forum specifically dedicated to guppies, though activity levels can vary.
*   **Facebook Groups**: Search for groups like "Guppy Breeders USA," "International Guppy Breeders," or groups specific to your region or particular guppy strains. These can be great for seeing photos, finding local breeders, and quick advice.
*   **Reddit**: Subreddits like r/Aquariums, r/Guppies, and r/PlantedTank offer diverse discussions and visual inspiration.

## A.2 Guppy Associations and Clubs

Joining a local or national guppy club can provide access to specialized knowledge, show opportunities, and high-quality breeding stock.

*   **International Fancy Guppy Association (IFGA - ifga.org)**: The premier organization for fancy guppy breeders and exhibitors in the United States and beyond. Their website lists sanctioned clubs and show schedules. Their judging standards are often the basis for guppy shows worldwide.
*   **World Guppy Association (WGA)**: Focuses on promoting guppy breeding globally, often with an emphasis on European and Asian standards.
*   **Local Aquarium Societies**: Many cities or regions have general aquarium societies that often have members specializing in livebearers or guppies. Check online for societies near you. These are excellent for local networking.

## A.3 Recommended Books (Beyond This One!)

While many modern resources are online, some classic and contemporary books offer deep dives.

*   **"Guppy Handbook" by Shirlie Sharpe (Barron's Educational Series)**: A good introductory guide covering basics.
*   **"The Complete Guide to Guppies" by Spencer Glass (TFH Publications)**: Often cited, though may be older, it contains foundational information.
*   **Books on Aquarium Fish Health**:
    *   **"Manual of Fish Health" by Dr. Chris Andrews, Adrian Exell, and Dr. Neville Carrington**: A comprehensive guide to fish diseases.
    *   **"Fish Disease: Diagnosis and Treatment" by Dr. Edward J. Noga**: A more technical and veterinary-focused resource, but highly authoritative.
*   **Books on Planted Aquariums**: If you're serious about lush planted tanks for your guppies:
    *   **"Ecology of the Planted Aquarium" by Diana Walstad**: A scientific yet accessible approach to low-tech planted tanks.
    *   **"Aquascaping: A Step-by-Step Guide to Planting, Styling, and Maintaining Beautiful Aquariums" by George Farmer**: Focuses on the artistic side of planted tanks.

## A.4 Reputable Online Guppy Suppliers and Breeders

When looking to purchase specific strains online, reputation is key. (Note: Listing specific breeders can be problematic as availability and quality change. This section provides guidance on *how* to find them.)

*   **AquaBid (aquabid.com)**: An auction site where many hobbyist breeders sell their fish. Check seller ratings and feedback carefully.
*   **Specialty Online Fish Stores**: Some larger online retailers specialize in livebearers or have dedicated guppy sections. Look for stores with good reviews, clear DOA (Dead On Arrival) policies, and good communication.
*   **Directly from Hobbyist Breeders**: Often found through forums, Facebook groups, or club contacts. This can be one of the best ways to get quality, healthy fish.
*   **Things to Look For in an Online Seller**:
    *   Clear photos/videos of the actual stock or representative stock.
    *   Detailed descriptions of the strain.
    *   Information on their water parameters.
    *   Positive reviews and testimonials.
    *   Responsive customer service.
    *   Clear shipping policies and DOA guarantee.

## A.5 Essential Aquarium Equipment and Supply Retailers

For tanks, filters, food, test kits, and medications:

*   **Aquarium Co-Op (aquariumcoop.com)**: Highly regarded for quality products (especially their own brand of food, Easy Green fertilizer, and sponge filters), excellent customer service, and informative YouTube channel.
*   **Bulk Reef Supply (bulkreefsupply.com)**: While focused on saltwater, they carry many high-quality freshwater supplies, especially filtration components and test kits.
*   **Amazon.com**: Wide selection from various brands, but quality can vary. Read reviews carefully.
*   **Local Fish Stores (LFS)**: Supporting your local, independent fish store is always encouraged. They can offer personalized advice and immediate access to supplies.

## A.6 YouTube Channels for Visual Learning

Many excellent YouTube channels provide visual guides, tank tours, and breeding tips.

*   **Aquarium Co-Op**: As mentioned, Cory McElroy and his team provide a wealth of practical, science-based fishkeeping advice.
*   **LRB Aquatics (Lucas Bretz)**: Focuses heavily on breeding, particularly livebearers and shrimp. Great DIY projects and practical tips.
*   **Simply Betta (but covers other fish too)**: Often has good information on fish health and care.
*   **George Farmer**: For high-end aquascaping inspiration, much of which can be adapted for beautiful guppy tanks.
*   **MD Fish Tanks**: Creative and often budget-friendly tank setups.

Search for specific topics like "guppy breeding setup," "DIY brine shrimp hatchery," or "treating ich in guppies" to find many helpful videos.

## A.7 Water Testing and Quality

*   **API Freshwater Master Test Kit**: Considered essential by many for accurately testing Ammonia, Nitrite, Nitrate, and pH.
*   **TDS Meter (Total Dissolved Solids)**: Useful for consistency, especially if you are trying to match water parameters for sensitive strains or using RO water.
*   **Seachem Prime**: A popular and effective water conditioner that detoxifies chlorine, chloramine, ammonia, and nitrite.
*   **Local Water Utility Report**: Your city or county water provider usually publishes an annual water quality report, which can give you baseline information about your tap water's pH and hardness.

This list is a starting point. The aquarium hobby is vast and filled with passionate individuals eager to share their knowledge. Don't hesitate to explore, ask questions, and contribute to the community. Happy fishkeeping!
